# Cashmere vs Mohair: A B2B Comparison of Cost, Feel, and Customer Perception

When sourcing luxury natural fibers, the choice between cashmere and mohair can significantly impact your product's cost, hand-feel, and market positioning. Both fibers originate from goats—cashmere from the undercoat of cashmere goats, and mohair from the Angora goat—but they differ dramatically in texture, durability, and price. For importers and brand buyers, understanding these differences is critical to making informed decisions that align with your target market and margin requirements.

In this guide, we provide a data-driven comparison of cashmere vs mohair, covering cost structures, tactile qualities, and consumer perception, with practical insights for B2B sourcing. ## 1. Fiber Basics: What Sets Cashmere and Mohair Apart

### Cashmere: The Soft Undercoat
Cashmere is the fine undercoat of cashmere goats (Capra hircus), harvested annually during the spring molt. Each goat produces only about 100–150 grams of usable cashmere, making it a rare and valuable commodity. The fiber diameter ranges from 14 to 19 microns, with premium grades often at 15.5 microns or below. Cashmere is renowned for its exceptional softness, lightweight warmth, and natural elasticity.

### Mohair: The Silky Long Hair
Mohair comes from the Angora goat, which is primarily raised in South Africa, Texas, and Turkey. Mohair fibers are longer (typically 25–40 cm) and coarser (25–40 microns) than cashmere. The fiber has a distinct luster and a smooth, silky texture, often described as having a 'halo' when brushed. Mohair is durable, resilient, and resistant to creasing, making it popular for outerwear, upholstery, and knitwear with a fuzzy finish.

## 2. Cost Analysis: Raw Fiber, Processing, and MOQ Implications

For B2B buyers, cost is a primary consideration. The price gap between cashmere and mohair is substantial, but processing costs, yields, and minimum order quantities (MOQs) also influence the final landed cost.

### Raw Fiber Costs
- **Cashmere:** The scarcity of cashmere drives its high price. Fine cashmere (15.5 µm) can command $70–$85 per kg, while coarser grades (17–19 µm) range from $50–$60 per kg. The best cashmere comes from Inner Mongolia, where the harsh climate produces the finest fibers.
- **Mohair:** Mohair is significantly cheaper, with raw fiber prices ranging from $5 to $15 per kg depending on quality and luster. Even premium kid mohair (first shearing) rarely exceeds $20 per kg.

### Processing and Yield
Cashmere requires dehairing to separate the fine undercoat from coarse guard hairs, a process that reduces yield by up to 30%. Mohair, being a single-coat fiber, does not require dehairing and has a higher yield. However, mohair's long staple length may require specialized spinning equipment, though most mills handle it easily.

### MOQ and Lead Times
- **Cashmere:** Because of its high value, cashmere yarns and fabrics are often produced in smaller batches. Our factory, for example, offers a MOQ of 300 kg per color for dyed cashmere yarn, with lead times of 3–4 weeks for standard orders.
- **Mohair:** Mohair's lower cost allows for larger MOQs, but many suppliers still require minimums of 500 kg per color. Lead times are similar, given the spinning and dyeing processes.

**Impact on Product Pricing:** A cashmere sweater (250 g) might cost $30–$50 in material alone, while a mohair sweater (300 g) could cost $5–$10. This price difference directly affects your retail pricing strategy and profit margins.

## 3. Feel and Performance: The Tactile Experience

### Hand-Feel and Comfort
Cashmere is synonymous with luxury softness. Its fine fibers bend easily, creating a plush, almost buttery feel against the skin. This makes it ideal for next-to-skin garments like scarves, shawls, and fine knitwear. However, cashmere can pill with friction, and it requires gentle care.

Mohair has a distinct, smooth texture with a natural sheen. While not as soft as cashmere, mohair is less itchy than wool and offers a unique 'fluffy' halo when brushed. It is often used in outerwear and cardigans where a structured, voluminous look is desired.

### Warmth and Breathability
Cashmere provides exceptional warmth without bulk—a 12-gauge cashmere sweater can be as warm as a 4-ply wool sweater. It also breathes well, regulating body temperature.

Mohair is also warm but less insulating per unit weight. Its hollow core (in some grades) offers good thermal regulation, but it is generally used in lighter garments or blended with wool for added warmth.

### Durability and Care
Cashmere is delicate; it can stretch, pill, and is prone to moth damage. It requires dry cleaning or hand washing with special detergents.

Mohair is stronger and more resilient. It resists creasing and pilling better than cashmere, making it more durable for everyday wear. Mohair garments can often be machine-washed on gentle cycles, though care labels vary.

## 4. Customer Perception: How End-Users View Each Fiber

### The Luxury Cachet of Cashmere
Cashmere has an unrivaled prestige in the luxury market. Consumers associate it with exclusivity, comfort, and high status. Brands like Loro Piana, Brunello Cucinelli, and Burberry have built their reputations on cashmere. As a B2B buyer, offering cashmere products can elevate your brand's perceived value and justify premium pricing.

### Mohair's Niche Appeal
Mohair is less known to the general public but is appreciated in fashion circles for its unique texture and luster. It is often used by designers for statement pieces. However, mohair's lower price point means it is perceived as a mid-tier fiber, not a high-luxury material. It can be positioned as an 'affordable luxury' or a sustainable alternative, given that Angora goats require less water and land than cashmere goats.

### Consumer Preferences by Region
- **East Asia (JP, KR, CN):** Strong preference for cashmere due to its softness and status. Mohair is less popular, except in avant-garde fashion.
- **Europe:** Both fibers are accepted; cashmere is seen as classic, while mohair is valued for its eco-credentials and unique aesthetics.
- **North America:** Cashmere dominates the luxury market, while mohair is used in casual and outdoor wear.

## 5. Blending: The Best of Both Worlds

Many B2B buyers choose to blend cashmere and mohair to balance cost and performance. A typical blend might be 70% cashmere, 30% mohair, or 50/50. Blending offers:

- **Cost Reduction:** Mohair lowers the overall material cost.
- **Enhanced Durability:** Mohair adds strength and reduces pilling.
- **Textural Interest:** Mohair adds a subtle sheen and halo, creating a unique hand-feel.

However, blending can also dilute cashmere's softness. It's crucial to test blends to ensure they meet your quality standards. Our R&D team can develop custom blends tailored to your specifications, with rigorous testing for pilling, abrasion, and colorfastness.

## 7. Making the Right Choice for Your Brand

### When to Choose Cashmere
- Your brand targets the luxury segment and commands premium prices.
- You require ultimate softness and lightweight warmth.
- You are willing to invest in high-quality raw materials to differentiate your products.

### When to Choose Mohair
- You need a cost-effective fiber with good durability.
- Your designs call for volume, luster, or a 'fluffy' aesthetic.
- You want to offer sustainable, low-impact natural fibers.

### When to Blend
- You want to balance cost and quality.
- You aim to create a unique texture that stands out in the market.
- You need to meet price points for mid-range collections.
