# Hand-Feel vs Micron Count: Why Two 15μm Cashmeres Can Feel Different (B2B Buyer's Quick Test)

You've just received two cashmere swatches from different suppliers. Both are labeled "15μm." Both claim to be Grade A. But when you rub them between your fingers, one feels buttery and soft, the other slightly prickly and thin. Which one is truly better? And more importantly, which one will your customers love enough to reorder?

As a B2B buyer, you know micron count matters—it's the industry's shorthand for fineness. But micron count is only one dimension of quality. Two lots of cashmere with identical average diameters can differ dramatically in hand feel due to fiber length, crimp, scale structure, and even the finishing chemistry applied during processing. This article explains why, and gives you a practical 5-minute sensory test you can run on any sample, no lab required.

## Why Micron Count Isn't the Whole Story

Micron count measures the average diameter of individual fibers. The lower the number, the finer the fiber, which generally translates to softer hand feel. The cashmere industry typically grades:

- **Under 15.5μm**: Extra-fine, luxury grade (used for high-end knitwear)
- **15.5–16.5μm**: Fine, standard premium
- **16.5–17.5μm**: Mid-grade, good for everyday luxury
- **Above 18μm**: Coarser, often blended or used for heavier garments

But here's the catch: **average diameter is a statistical measure**. A lot with an average of 15μm could contain fibers ranging from 10μm to 20μm. If the distribution is wide, the coarser fibers will poke out and cause itching, even though the average looks great on paper.

### The Hidden Variables: Length, Crimp, and Scale

Three other fiber properties significantly affect hand feel:

1. **Fiber Length (Staple Length)**: Longer fibers (35–40mm) produce smoother, stronger yarns. Short fibers (under 30mm) create a fuzzy, pilling-prone surface that feels less luxurious. Two lots with the same micron count can have very different staple lengths.

2. **Crimp**: Natural cashmere has a wavy crimp that gives yarn elasticity and a soft, airy texture. Crimp frequency and amplitude vary by animal and season. More crimp = better bulk and a springier, more comfortable feel.

3. **Scale Structure**: Cashmere scales are less pronounced than wool's, which is why it feels smoother. But scale density and orientation differ among fibers. Denser, more raised scales can cause slight roughness even at the same diameter.

### Processing and Finishing: The Invisible Factor

Even if the raw fiber is identical, how it's processed changes hand feel dramatically. Chemical treatments (e.g., chlorine-based anti-shrink, softeners, silicone finishes) can mask or enhance softness. Some suppliers add heavy silicone to make low-quality fiber feel silky—but that softness washes out after a few cycles. Others use eco-friendly enzymatic treatments that preserve natural softness.

## The B2B Buyer's Quick Sensory Test

You don't need a lab to catch major hand-feel discrepancies. This 5-minute test will help you compare samples objectively. Use it before you commit to a bulk order.

### What You Need

- Two or more cashmere samples (yarn skein or fabric swatch)
- A clean, dry, neutral-smelling room (avoid perfume or coffee)
- A notepad to record scores

### Step 1: The Rub Test (30 seconds)

Take each sample and rub it gently between your thumb and index finger for 10 strokes. Note:

- **Smoothness**: Does it glide, or does it catch?
- **Prickle**: Any sharp sensations? This indicates coarser fibers protruding.
- **Warmth**: Does it feel warm after rubbing? Cashmere should generate slight warmth due to its insulating air pockets.

Score 1–5 (5 = best).

### Step 2: The Squeeze Test (20 seconds)

Crumple the sample in your fist for 10 seconds, then release. Observe:

- **Recovery**: Does it spring back quickly? Slow recovery suggests poor crimp or excessive fiber breakage.
- **Bulk**: Does it feel dense and full, or flat and lifeless? Bulk indicates good crimp and fiber length.

Score 1–5.

### Step 3: The Cheek Test (10 seconds)

Gently press the sample against the inside of your wrist or cheek—areas with sensitive skin. Move it in a small circle. Note any itching or roughness. This simulates how the garment will feel against the neck or face.

Score 1–5.

### Step 4: The Visual Check (1 minute)

Hold the sample up to natural light. Look for:

- **Fuzziness**: Excessive loose fibers on the surface indicate short staple length or poor spinning.
- **Evenness**: Are there thick/thin spots? Inconsistent yarn density affects feel.
- **Luster**: Cashmere should have a soft, natural sheen—not dull or artificially shiny.

Score 1–5.

### Step 5: The Wash Test (optional, but recommended)

If you have time, wash a small swatch in lukewarm water with mild detergent, air dry, then repeat the rub and cheek tests. This reveals whether softness is durable or just a surface finish. Many buyers skip this—but it's the difference between a one-season product and a loyal customer.

## Interpreting Your Results

Add up the scores (max 25). Here's a rough guide:

| Score | Interpretation | Action |
|-------|----------------|--------|
| 20–25 | Excellent hand feel | Proceed with confidence; verify other specs (micron, length) via lab report |
| 15–19 | Good, but room for improvement | Ask supplier about fiber length and finishing process |
| <15   | Poor; likely issues with fiber quality or processing | Reject or request a new sample with detailed specs |

Keep in mind: this test is subjective. Use it to flag differences, not to certify absolute quality. For final decisions, request a **fiber analysis report** from an accredited lab (e.g., Intertek, SGS) that includes:

- Average micron and distribution (CV%)
- Staple length
- Crimp frequency
- Scale density (if available)

## How to Specify Hand Feel in Your Purchase Contract

To avoid disappointment, don't just write "15μm" in your contract. Add these specifications:

- **Micron range**: e.g., 14.5–15.5μm (with a max CV of 22%)
- **Minimum staple length**: e.g., 34mm (for worsted yarn)
- **Crimp count**: e.g., 3–5 crimps per cm
- **Finishing**: Specify "no silicone softener" or "eco-friendly softening" if you want natural hand feel
- **Hand feel standard**: Reference a physical benchmark sample (sealed between buyer and supplier)

A reliable supplier will welcome these specs—they show you know what you're doing. If they push back, that's a red flag.

## Common Myths About Cashmere Hand Feel

Let's debunk a few misconceptions that can lead buyers astray:

- **Myth: Lower micron always means softer.**
  Not if the fiber is short or damaged. A 14μm fiber that's 28mm long will feel rougher than a 15μm fiber that's 38mm long.

- **Myth: Softness is all about the fiber.**
  Processing matters just as much. Over-bleaching, harsh chemicals, and aggressive carding can break fibers and ruin hand feel.

- **Myth: You can judge quality by price alone.**
  While ultra-cheap cashmere is suspect, the most expensive isn't always the best. You're paying for brand, not necessarily fiber quality.

- **Myth: Hand feel is impossible to standardize.**
  With proper fiber specs and a sealed benchmark, you can absolutely set measurable standards.
