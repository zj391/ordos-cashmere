# Cashmere Scarf vs Pashmina Shawl: 8 Differences B2B Buyers Need to Know

A cashmere scarf and a pashmina shawl are often sold in the same product display case — but they are not the same product. The price, hand-feel, target customer, and even the manufacturing process differ. Sourcing managers who don't know these differences lose margin on underpriced orders or oversell scarves to customers expecting shawl-grade hand-feel.

This guide breaks down the 8 differences that matter for B2B buyers, with sourcing numbers from a 23-year Ordos cashmere factory.

## 1. Fiber Origin

**Cashmere scarf**: Made from cashmere goat underdown. Grade A Mongolian cashmere runs 14.5–15.5μm. Source: Alashan and Ordos plateau herders.

**Pashmina shawl**: Originally a regional term from Kashmir (India/Pakistan) for cashmere from the Changthangi goat. Today the term is used loosely — most "pashmina" shawls sold globally are made from Chinese or Mongolian cashmere 14.5–15.5μm, the same fiber as cashmere scarves.

**B2B implication**: When a customer asks for "pashmina," clarify whether they want authentic Kashmir-grade (Changthangi goat, ~12–14μm, 3-5x the price of standard cashmere) or commercial pashmina-style (Mongolian cashmere, 14.5–15.5μm, similar price to cashmere scarves).

## 2. Dimensions

**Cashmere scarf**: Standard 180–190 cm long × 35–45 cm wide. Designed to wrap once or twice around the neck.

**Pashmina shawl**: Standard 200–210 cm long × 70–100 cm wide. Designed to drape over shoulders or wrap twice around the neck with volume.

**B2B implication**: A shawl uses 2–3x more fabric than a scarf. Finished weight per piece is correspondingly higher. When calculating freight or display space, account for the size difference.

## 3. Weight Per Piece

**Cashmere scarf**: 60–120 g (typical), depending on length and yarn weight (2/26 to 2/48 Nm).

**Pashmina shawl**: 180–280 g (typical) for the wider 200×70 cm format. The 200×100 cm "wrap" format runs 240–320 g.

**B2B implication**: A 100-piece order of pashmina shawls ships ~20 kg; the same order in cashmere scarves ships ~10 kg. Freight cost scales accordingly.

## 4. Weave and Yarn Structure

**Cashmere scarf**: Plain weave (1/1) or twill, woven on Toyota JAT810 rapier looms. Yarn typically 2/48 Nm worsted-spun for a smooth, refined surface.

**Pashmina shawl**: Plain weave (1/1) or twill at larger widths. Yarn typically 2/26 Nm woolen-spun for loftiness and softness.

**B2B implication**: Worsted-spun scarves look dressier and read better printed; woolen-spun shawls feel loftier and warmer. The yarn structure choice depends on your customer.

## 5. Hand-Feel

**Cashmere scarf**: Smooth, refined, dressy. The 12-gauge worsted construction reads as a luxury accessory.

**Pashmina shawl**: Soft, lofty, warm. The woolen-spun construction gives a more relaxed, casual feel.

**B2B implication**: Marketing copy must match the hand-feel. Selling a woolen-spun shawl as "dressy" will create returns; selling a worsted-spun scarf as "blanket-soft" will underwhelm.

## 6. Pricing

**Cashmere scarf**: USD 8–22 per piece FOB Tianjin at 100–500 piece MOQ, depending on size, yarn, and customization.

**Pashmina shawl**: USD 12–35 per piece FOB Tianjin at 100–500 piece MOQ, depending on size, yarn, and customization.

**B2B implication**: Shawls price 30–60% higher than scarves at the same yarn quality, due to the 2–3x fabric usage. Retail markup typically 5–8x for both, but shawls hit higher absolute price points ($150–500) and higher perceived value.

## 7. MOQ and Customization

**Cashmere scarf**: MOQ 100 pcs per design in stock colors. Custom Pantone from 5 kg per color. Custom size from 100 pcs. Custom digital print from 200 pcs.

**Pashmina shawl**: MOQ 100 pcs per design in stock colors. Custom Pantone from 5 kg per color. Custom size from 100 pcs (180×60 cm to 220×90 cm range). Custom print from 200 pcs.

**B2B implication**: Same MOQ structure for both. Larger shawl orders (300+ pcs) qualify for the best per-piece pricing because the mill can optimize loom setup.

## 8. Lead Time

**Cashmere scarf**: 25–30 days bulk + 3–5 days QC + 20–35 days sea freight to EU/US.

**Pashmina shawl**: 25–30 days bulk + 3–5 days QC + 20–35 days sea freight. Same production lead time, but the wider fabric requires more careful loom setup, so first-time orders may add 5–7 days for sampling.

## Which One Should You Buy?

**Cashmere scarf**: Best for retailers targeting office wear, business casual, or customers who want a refined luxury accessory. Higher turnover per piece, lower price point, smaller display footprint.

**Pashmina shawl**: Best for retailers targeting evening wear, gift giving, or customers who want warmth and visual volume. Higher absolute price point, larger display footprint, higher margin per piece.

**Both**: Many retailers carry both, with the scarf as the entry-level cashmere and the shawl as the upgrade.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is pashmina a type of cashmere?

Originally yes — "pashmina" was a regional term from Kashmir for cashmere fiber from the Changthangi goat. Today the term is used loosely. Most "pashmina" shawls sold globally are made from Chinese or Mongolian cashmere 14.5–15.5μm, the same fiber as cashmere scarves. Authentic Kashmir pashmina (Changthangi goat, 12–14μm) is rare and 3-5x the price.

### What is the difference between a pashmina shawl and a cashmere wrap?

A pashmina shawl is typically 200×70 cm. A cashmere wrap is typically 200×100 cm or larger. Wraps use more fabric and provide full shoulder coverage for evening wear. Wraps price 15–25% higher than shawls at the same yarn quality.

### Can I get a pashmina shawl with custom logo?

Yes. Custom logo options include woven labels (sewn into the seam), printed labels, embroidered logos, custom hangtags, and custom packaging. Woven labels are the most common for cashmere shawls. MOQ 100 pcs per design with woven label integration.

### How do I verify a pashmina shawl is genuine cashmere?

Three tests: (1) burn test — cashmere fiber burns slowly with a smell like burnt hair and leaves a crushable black ash; (2) microscope test — cashmere has a smooth scale pattern visible under 40x magnification; (3) micron count test — laboratory equipment measures fiber diameter; genuine cashmere is below 19μm. Wool or synthetic fibers will fail one or more of these tests.
