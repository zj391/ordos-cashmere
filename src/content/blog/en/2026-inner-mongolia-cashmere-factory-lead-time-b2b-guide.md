# 2026 Inner Mongolia Cashmere Factory Lead Time: A B2B Production Calendar from Ordos

Most B2B buyers plan their cashmere program around the **retail delivery window** — the date a sweater or scarf needs to be in a store for the autumn or holiday season. They then ask a factory: *"Can you hit this date?"* and accept the answer at face value. What they rarely see is the **production calendar behind that answer** — the spinning slots, the dyeing windows, the knitting-program allocation, the finishing capacity, and the inspection slots that have to line up for a yes to be true.

This note is the **factory-side companion** to our [2026 Inner Mongolia cashmere harvest season field guide](/en/blog/2026-inner-mongolia-cashmere-harvest-season-b2b-guide/). Where the harvest guide covers April–August on the pasture side, this one covers September–March inside our Ordos facility. It is written for B2B procurement, brand, and sourcing teams who need to understand **why some lead times are firm and some are not**, and how to plan backward from a retail delivery date with confidence.

## Why Ordos factory lead times are not a single number

When a buyer asks "what is your lead time?", the honest answer is usually a range — and the range reflects four underlying constraints, not factory efficiency:

1. **Yarn availability**: Dehaired fiber from the May–June auction round has to be spun into the required count, ply, twist and color. Spinning capacity is finite, and a mill's autumn spinning slots are allocated first to programs with confirmed purchase orders.
2. **Dyeing window**: Yarn-dye lots and garment-dye lots use different equipment and different capacity calendars. Yarn-dyed programs lock color 60–90 days before knitting; garment-dyed programs can run closer to the knitting date but are limited by dye-house capacity in any given week.
3. **Knitting-program allocation**: A factory's knitting machines are configured for a specific gauge range (e.g., 12-gauge, 14-gauge, 16-gauge) and stitch structure. Switching gauge or major stitch pattern between programs costs setup time that the buyer doesn't see in the lead-time quote.
4. **Finishing, mending, and pre-shipment inspection**: Washing, softening, pressing, mending, and final AQL inspection are skilled-labor steps with their own weekly capacity. A pre-shipment inspection slot is typically booked 7–14 days before ex-works date.

A buyer who treats "lead time" as a single number is treating four constraints as one. A buyer who understands the four constraints can plan around each one.

## The September–March production calendar at our Ordos facility

The following is the production calendar we run each year, observed at our facility and across the wider Ordos cashmere-manufacturing cluster. **It is a structural calendar, not a 2026-specific forecast** — the same shape repeats every year, with one-to-two-week shifts driven by Spring Festival date and the May–June auction timing.

### September–October: yarn-spinning peak

The Ordos and Bayannur spinning mills move into their **highest-capacity period** in September–October. Two reasons: dehaired fiber from the May–July auction round has been pooled and graded, and knitting programs are queueing for autumn yarn deliveries. **For a buyer, this is the moment to confirm yarn-spinning slots**, not to wait for the mill to be free.

What a buyer should do in September–October:

- **Issue a purchase order with deposit** for any program that wants March–May 2027 retail delivery. Yarn-locked programs take priority over inquiries in the mill's allocation.
- **Confirm yarn specification in writing** — count (Nm), ply, micron, color, twist direction, oil content. "Premium 2/48nm" is a discussion point, not a specification.
- **Confirm fiber-lot traceability paperwork** — OEKO-TEX, GCS, RWS, the Inner Mongolia GI, or your customer's house standard. The documentation chain starts at the dehaired-lot level and cannot be backfilled at the finished-garment stage.

### November: dyeing peak

Yarn-dyed programs move into the **dye-house peak in November**. The dye houses in the Ordos cluster run their largest lots of the year between late October and mid-December, ahead of the Spring Festival shipping cutoff. **Garment-dyed programs** (where color is applied after knitting) can run later, but are constrained by dye-house capacity after the New Year.

What a buyer should know about November:

- **Yarn-dyed color approval has to be locked by mid-October** for an autumn knitting start. Late-November color approval pushes the program into the New Year.
- **Custom Pantone matches** require lab-dip approval (typically 7–14 days) before bulk dyeing starts. A buyer who asks for a custom color in December is asking for the dye house to add their lot to a January backlog.
- **Color fastness testing** (wash, light, rub) is usually run on the lab-dip or first bulk lot and adds 5–10 days to the timeline.

### December–January: knitting-program peak and Spring Festival stop

December is the **knitting-program peak** in our Ordos facility and across most of the cluster. Programs that locked yarn in September and color in November are now in full production. January is interrupted by **Spring Festival (Chinese New Year)**, which in 2027 falls on February 6. The practical impact:

- **Production effectively stops 7–10 days before the Spring Festival date** for travel, factory shutdown, and machine maintenance.
- **Production restarts 7–14 days after the Spring Festival date**, depending on worker return and supplier reopening.
- **The Spring Festival window is non-negotiable** — it is the single largest calendar constraint in any China-based cashmere program, and any 2026/2027 plan that crosses it needs explicit acknowledgement from the factory.

What a buyer should do in December–January:

- **Treat Spring Festival as a hard lead-time boundary.** Any program that needs to ship between late January and mid-February is at material risk of missing the date.
- **Approve pre-production hand-feel samples before the Spring Festival** so knitting can resume immediately after the holiday.
- **Do not place new orders in the last 10 days before the Spring Festival** unless you accept the production start date in late February or later.

### February–March: knitting resume and finishing ramp

Knitting resumes across the cluster in **mid-to-late February**. Finishing (washing, softening, mending, pressing) and pre-shipment inspection ramp up through March. **For a buyer, this is the window when late-spring and early-summer 2027 deliveries should be cleared** — not started.

What a buyer should know about February–March:

- **Pre-shipment inspection slots book out 7–14 days ahead.** A buyer who needs an inspection on March 20 should confirm the slot by early March.
- **Final AQL inspection** (typically 2.5 AQL for major defects, 4.0 AQL for minor defects, per ISO 2859-1) is the last gate before ex-works delivery. Plan for 2–5 days of inspection time depending on lot size.
- **Shipping documents** — commercial invoice, packing list, certificate of origin, fiber-lot certificates, OEKO-TEX or GCS certificates, bill of lading — are prepared in parallel with inspection, not after.

## Backward planning: a worked example for 2027 spring delivery

A common B2B buyer request is: "I want 1,000 pieces of 14.5μm 2/48nm women's crewneck sweaters in our custom Pantone, delivered to a Hamburg DC by mid-March 2027 for an April retail floor." Here is what that timeline actually requires when worked backward from the retail date.

| Working backward | Date | What has to happen |
|---|---|---|
| Retail floor | **Mid-March 2027** | Goods on shelf or in DC |
| Hamburg DC delivery | **Early March 2027** | Sea-freight transit (typically 28–35 days Tianjin → Hamburg) + EU customs clearance |
| Ex-works Ordos | **Late January 2027** | Goods ready at Ordos factory gate |
| Pre-shipment inspection | **Mid-January 2027** | AQL inspection, document prep |
| Finishing complete | **Early January 2027** | Washing, softening, mending, pressing |
| Knitting complete | **Mid-December 2026** | All knitting done, surplus yarn and labels on hand |
| Knitting starts | **Early November 2026** | Yarn and color locked, knitting slot allocated |
| **Color approval** | **Mid-September 2026** | Lab-dip approved, bulk dyeing scheduled |
| Yarn spinning | **August–September 2026** | Fiber pooled, yarn spun to spec |
| **Purchase order with deposit** | **July–August 2026** | Yarn-spinning slot allocated at the mill |

What this timeline shows: **the buyer's last actionable decision is yarn-locking in July–August 2026** — five months before the yarn even reaches the knitting machine, and **eight months before the retail floor date**. A buyer who delays the PO to October has already missed the spring 2027 delivery window; the realistic next slot is summer 2027.

## What a "rush" lead time actually means

Almost every B2B conversation eventually includes a "rush" or "express" request. Here is what is realistically possible from our Ordos facility, and what is not:

- **Stock-yarn, stock-color, simple knit (12-gauge or 14-gauge jersey or rib)**: 25–35 days ex-works from PO. Possible because yarn and color are already in the warehouse. **This is the only realistic "rush" tier.**
- **Stock-yarn, custom Pantone**: 35–50 days. Yarn is ready but dyeing adds 2–3 weeks.
- **Custom yarn, stock color**: 50–70 days. Spinning a new yarn lot is the bottleneck.
- **Custom yarn, custom color**: 70–110 days. Both spinning and dyeing have to be sequenced.
- **Custom yarn, custom color, complex knit (e.g., 16-gauge intarsia, cabling, fine gauge with shaping)**: 90–140 days. The knitting machine allocation becomes the binding constraint.

What is **not** realistically possible regardless of rush:

- Spring Festival-crossing programs that need to ship in the last 10 days before or first 10 days after the holiday.
- Custom baby cashmere (<13.5μm) lots outside the September–October combing window.
- Programs that require fiber from a specific pasture or herder cooperative outside the May–June combing round.

A buyer who asks for any of these as a "rush" is asking for an answer that no factory in Ordos can honestly give.

## How to pressure-test a factory's lead-time quote

When a factory gives you a lead-time quote — whether 30 days, 60 days, or 90 days — five questions separate a real commitment from a wishful one:

1. **"What yarn-spinning slot are you allocating?"** A real answer names the mill and the week. A vague answer ("we'll spin it") is not a slot.
2. **"What is the lab-dip and color-approval timeline?"** A real answer gives the day count. A vague answer hides the dye-house bottleneck.
3. **"How does the Spring Festival affect this program?"** A real answer gives the specific shutdown and restart dates for the facility. A vague answer means the program will slip.
4. **"What is the pre-shipment inspection slot?"** A real answer names the inspection date. A vague answer ("we'll inspect before shipping") is not an AQL plan.
5. **"What documentation chain is included?"** A real answer lists the certificates, the test reports, and the origin documents. A vague answer means these will be backfilled — or skipped.

A factory that answers all five clearly is giving you a real commitment. A factory that answers one or two vaguely is giving you a wishful number.
