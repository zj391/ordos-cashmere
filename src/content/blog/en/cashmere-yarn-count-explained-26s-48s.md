## What Does 'Yarn Count' Mean in Cashmere?

Yarn count is a measure of fineness or thickness of a yarn. In the metric system, it's expressed as Nm (number of meters per gram). So, **26s (Nm 26/2)** means that 1 gram of yarn contains 26 meters of single-ply yarn, and the '/2' indicates it's two-ply (two single yarns twisted together). Similarly, **48s (Nm 48/2)** means 1 gram of yarn contains 48 meters of single-ply yarn, making it much finer.

In simple terms: **the higher the count, the finer the yarn, and the lighter and more delicate the resulting fabric.**

### Why Does Count Matter in B2B Sourcing?

For B2B buyers, yarn count directly affects:

- **Fabric weight** (grams per square meter, GSM)
- **Hand feel and drape**
- **Durability and pilling resistance**
- **Production cost and yield**
- **End-use applications** (e.g., lightweight summer knits vs. heavy winter sweaters)

## 26s Cashmere Yarn: Characteristics and Applications

26s is a medium-to-heavy weight yarn, typically used for **chunky knits, outerwear, and accessories** that require warmth and structure. It's a versatile workhorse in the cashmere industry.

### Key Specifications

- **Fineness:** ~26 Nm per ply, two-ply makes a robust yarn
- **Typical fabric weight:** 7–12 GG (gauge) knits, resulting in fabrics weighing 300–500 GSM
- **Micron count:** Usually 15.5–16.5 microns (depending on raw material grade)
- **Yield:** Lower yield per kg of raw cashmere compared to finer counts

### Advantages

- **Durability:** Thicker yarns are more resistant to abrasion and pilling, making them ideal for high-wear items.
- **Faster production:** Heavier yarns knit faster, reducing labor costs per unit.
- **Cost-effective:** Less processing required, and raw material usage is lower per meter of fabric (in terms of length).
- **Structured drape:** Creates garments with a more substantial, tailored silhouette.

### Best Applications

- **Men's and women's sweaters** (classic crewnecks, cardigans)
- **Coats and jackets** (woven or knitted)
- **Scarves, blankets, and throws**
- **Loungewear and heavy knitwear**

### Typical B2B Use Case

A European importer sourcing **heavy-gauge knitwear for a winter collection** might choose 26s to achieve a chunky, premium feel. With a 26s yarn, you can knit at 3–5 gauge, creating a dense, warm fabric that stands up to everyday wear.

## 48s Cashmere Yarn: Characteristics and Applications

48s is a fine yarn that produces **lightweight, luxurious fabrics** with a soft, fluid drape. It's the go-to choice for **high-end fashion, delicate knits, and year-round wear**.

### Key Specifications

- **Fineness:** ~48 Nm per ply, two-ply makes a very fine yarn
- **Typical fabric weight:** 12–16 GG knits, resulting in fabrics weighing 150–250 GSM
- **Micron count:** Usually 14.5–15.5 microns (premium grade)
- **Yield:** Higher yield per kg of raw cashmere (more meters of yarn per gram)

### Advantages

- **Exceptional softness:** Fine yarns feel incredibly smooth against the skin.
- **Lightweight comfort:** Ideal for layering and transitional seasons.
- **Elegant drape:** Creates garments that flow beautifully.
- **Higher perceived value:** Fine-gauge knits often command premium prices.

### Best Applications

- **Lightweight sweaters and cardigans** (women's and men's)
- **Turtlenecks, polos, and fine-gauge t-shirts**
- **Knit dresses and skirts**
- **Scarves and shawls** (lightweight)
- **Baby and children's wear** (if superfine)

### Typical B2B Use Case

A Japanese brand focusing on **minimalist, year-round knitwear** might opt for 48s to produce a fine-gauge sweater that can be worn under a jacket. The lightweight nature allows for extended selling seasons, reducing inventory risk.

## How to Choose: A Decision Framework for B2B Buyers

When deciding between 26s and 48s, ask yourself these questions:

### 1. What is your target market and price point?
- **Luxury, high-end**: 48s aligns with premium positioning.
- **Mid-range, everyday luxury**: 26s offers durability and cost-effectiveness.

### 2. What is the garment's intended use?
- **Heavy winter outerwear** → 26s
- **Year-round lightweight layers** → 48s
- **Accessories like scarves** → Both, depending on desired weight

### 3. What is your production capacity?
- **Fine-gauge knitting** requires more knitting time and skilled labor. If you have high-volume needs, 26s might be easier to scale.
- **48s yarn** is more delicate and may require slower knitting speeds to avoid defects.

### 4. What is your climate?
- **Cold climates** (e.g., Northern Europe, Northeast Asia) favor heavier weights (26s).
- **Mild climates** (e.g., Japan, coastal regions) favor lighter weights (48s).

### 5. What is your supply chain lead time?
- **26s yarn** is typically produced faster due to less processing.
- **48s yarn** may require longer lead times, especially if you need premium micron counts.

## Practical Tips for Testing and Sampling
