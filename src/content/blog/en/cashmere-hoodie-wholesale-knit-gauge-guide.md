# Cashmere Hoodie Wholesale Guide: Knit Gauge, Yarn Count & Construction Specs for B2B Buyers

Cashmere hoodies occupy a specific corner of the cashmere market — too casual for a traditional V-neck cardigan buyer, too refined for an athleisure program. The B2B buyers who do well with cashmere hoodies are usually building one of three product lines: premium athleisure (¥800–2,500 retail), boutique layering pieces ($180–450 retail), or corporate-casual uniform programs (where the hood replaces a formal collar). Each program has different specs, different MOQ requirements, and different risk profiles.

This guide walks through the engineering and sourcing decisions that determine whether a cashmere hoodie program succeeds at your retail tier. It is written from the perspective of an Ordos facility that produces both jacquard cable-knit hoodies and simpler flat-knit hoodies for private-label buyers.

## Cashmere Hoodie vs Cashmere Crewneck Pullover: When to Add the Hood

A hoodie adds roughly $4–8 to the wholesale unit cost versus a comparable crewneck pullover. The hood (with or without drawcord) requires:

- **An additional knitted piece** (the hood itself) — typically 60–90g for a cashmere hood
- **A drawcord channel** — usually a contrasting cashmere cord with metal or fabric tips
- **Front pocket integration** if specified (kangaroo or side-seam welt pockets add $1.50–3.00)
- **Button-front or zip-front closure** as an alternative to the pullover — adds $2.50–5.50

If your retail program is already selling cashmere crewnecks at $180+ retail, the marginal cost of adding a hooded version is small relative to the consumer-perceived novelty. If you are below $120 retail, the hood cost premium is harder to absorb.

## Construction: Pullover vs Zip-Front vs Button-Front

Three hoodie constructions are common in cashmere:

**Pullover hoodie** (no front closure): the simplest and most common. Wholesale $32–42 per piece, MOQ 100. Best for boutique layering programs.

**Button-front hoodie** (full button placket, 5–7 buttons): more structured look, blends hoodie aesthetics with cardigan styling. Wholesale $36–48 per piece, MOQ 100. Best for transitional-season retail and corporate-casual uniform programs.

**Zip-front hoodie**: requires metal or plastic zipper integration, more complex construction. Wholesale $38–52 per piece, MOQ 200. Best for premium athleisure where the zip is a brand feature.

## Knit Gauge and Yarn Count

**Gauge 5gg – 7gg (chunky)**: 380–480g hoodie weight. Visible cable-knit or jacquard texture, drapes heavily, premium gift-set aesthetic. The most common gauge for cable-knit cashmere hoodies.

**Gauge 9gg – 12gg (mid)**: 280–360g hoodie weight. Smooth or lightly patterned surface, balanced structure. Standard for commercial cashmere hoodie production.

**Gauge 14gg (fine)**: 220–280g hoodie weight. Smooth surface, refined drape, dressier aesthetic. Common for athleisure-tier cashmere hoodies sold at premium retail.

**Yarn count**: 2/26 Nm woolen-spun for chunky knits (most common); 2/48 Nm worsted-spun for fine-gauge pieces (premium tier). Worsted-spun yarn adds 30–40% to the yarn cost.

## Common Mistakes B2B Buyers Make

**Mistake 1: Specifying a fine-gauge 14gg hoodie for a gift-set program.** The yarn becomes too thin to express cable-knit or jacquard patterns. Use 5gg – 9gg for pattern programs; reserve 14gg for plain-knit athleisure.

**Mistake 2: Forgetting to specify hood lining.** A single-layer cashmere hood against bare skin can feel scratchy on sensitive users. A double-layer hood or jersey lining adds $1.50–2.50 per piece and dramatically improves wearer comfort.

**Mistake 3: Underestimating the drawcord cost.** Premium metal or leather-tipped drawcords add $1.20–2.80 per piece. For 1,000-piece orders this becomes a meaningful line item.

**Mistake 4: Skipping sample fitting on size S and XXL.** Cashmere hoodie sizing is more forgiving than woven garments, but the hood-to-shoulder drop still varies. Always request size-run samples before bulk production.
