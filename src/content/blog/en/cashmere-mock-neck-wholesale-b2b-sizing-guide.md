# Cashmere Mock Neck Sweater Wholesale: B2B Spec, Sizing & MOQ Guide

The cashmere mock neck sits in the small but growing segment between crew neck and full turtleneck. It covers the throat without bunching, layers cleanly under a jacket without bulk, and reads as more polished than a crew without committing to the formality of a full turtleneck. For B2B buyers building FW collections at the $180–340 retail tier, the mock neck has become a quiet best-seller — not as universal as the crew, but with higher average order value.

This guide walks through the mock depth, gauge, sizing, and finishing decisions that determine whether a cashmere mock neck program succeeds.

## Mock Depth Specification

The mock depth (collar height when worn) determines the mock's character:

**3cm shallow mock**: reads as a thicker crew neck. Refined, business-appropriate. Best for office-wear programs.

**4cm standard mock**: the most-requested depth. Versatile, layers cleanly, works under a jacket or alone. The default for FW 2026.

**5cm deep mock**: bolder, more fashion-forward. Reads as a half-turtleneck. Suits boutique and editorial retail.

**6cm+ deep mock**: rare for cashmere; reads as a shortened turtleneck. Risk of bunching under a jacket.

The mock depth specification in the bulk contract should include the **worn height** (3cm, 4cm, 5cm) and the **flat knit height** (5cm, 6cm, 7cm) — the difference accounts for the fold behavior.

## Construction Details

**Collar stand**: knit as a separate piece, joined to the body with a linking seam. The seam must be flat (no bulk against the throat) and recover fully after washing.

**Collar fold behavior**: should fold cleanly without flipping or rolling. Specify the fold line in the pattern piece (typically 2–3cm from the top edge).

**Body gauge vs collar gauge**: typically 1 gauge finer for the collar (12gg body, 14gg collar) to give the collar a tighter, more polished look. Mismatched gauge is a common source of bulk and uneven fold behavior.

**Cuff and hem**: standard 2×2 rib, 6–8cm width. Matching body gauge or one gauge finer.

## Knit Gauge and Yarn

**Gauge 7gg – 9gg (chunky)**: 320–420g mock neck weight, visible knit texture. Best for gift-set programs and FW layering collections.

**Gauge 10gg – 12gg (mid)**: 260–320g mock neck weight. The most-requested gauge for B2B programs.

**Gauge 14gg (fine)**: 220–280g mock neck weight. Refined, dressier aesthetic. Common for office-wear programs.

**Yarn**: 2/26 Nm woolen-spun for most programs; 2/48 Nm worsted-spun for premium tier (adds 30–40% yarn cost).

## Common Mistakes B2B Buyers Make

**Mistake 1: Specifying the same gauge for body and collar.** This creates a bulky, unflattering collar. The collar should be one gauge finer for the cleanest fold.

**Mistake 2: Skipping the fold-behavior test.** The mock depth specification only matters if the fold behaves correctly. Always test a sample collar under live conditions (washed and worn for 30 minutes) before bulk production.

**Mistake 3: Forgetting to specify collar recovery target.** Cashmere collars stretch with wear. Specify 90%+ recovery after 30 minutes in the bulk contract.

**Mistake 4: Underestimating the collar yarn consumption.** The collar stand adds 15–25g of yarn per mock neck versus a crew neck. Budget this for both cost and lead-time.

**Mistake 5: Specifying a 5cm+ mock for office-wear programs.** The deep mock reads as too casual for office settings. Reserve 5cm+ for boutique and editorial retail.
