# Cashmere Shipping & Import Guide: FOB, CIF, Duties, Lead Times, and Documents

This **cashmere import guide** explains how an Ordos order reaches a US or EU warehouse, which documents support clearance, and where landed-cost errors occur. **All freight rates, lead times, and duty figures are indicative 2026 planning ranges—not quotes, tariff rulings, or legal/tax advice. Confirm the current position with a licensed customs broker and forwarder.**

## Executive Summary

FOB is common for experienced buyers; CIF bundles freight and minimum insurance; DDP can suit selected low-volume programs. Indicative **cashmere shipping from China** transit is 30–38 days by sea, 5–9 by air, and 3–5 by courier. Duty depends on HS code, composition, construction, origin, and destination. Confirm HTSUS or TARIC classification before fixing a landed-cost budget.

## FOB vs CIF vs DDP — Comparison Table

The three Incoterms differ on three things: who books freight, who buys insurance, and who clears customs.

| Dimension | FOB | CIF | DDP |
|---|---|---|---|
| Risk transfers | Origin port loading | Origin port loading | Buyer warehouse |
| Freight booking | Buyer | Factory | Factory |
| Insurance | Buyer | Factory (Incoterms 2020 minimum cover; higher cover by agreement) | Factory |
| Destination clearance | Buyer broker | Buyer broker | Factory broker |
| Duties & VAT at destination | Buyer | Buyer | Factory |
| Best for | Buyers with a forwarder | One bundled landed number | First-time, small drops |
| Relative use (factory planning example) | Most common | Some orders | Small minority |

For **FOB vs CIF cashmere**, FOB gives the buyer control of freight; CIF lets the seller arrange freight and minimum-cover insurance while the buyer still clears customs and pays destination charges. DDP requires a confirmed HS code, importer-of-record arrangement, and capable destination broker.

## Required Shipping Documents for Cashmere Exports

A complete **cashmere shipping documents** package has two buckets. Customs-required documents (commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading / airway bill, CoO, and where applicable the security filing) clear the goods. Buyer / compliance certificates (fiber-lot / OEKO-TEX / GOTS / RWS, voluntary origin certificates) are typically not mandated by customs on knitwear but are required by the buyer or brand's quality program. We routinely prepare:

1. **Commercial Invoice** — buyer, seller, HS code, origin, value, Incoterm, payment terms.
2. **Packing List** — carton count, weights, dimensions, garment breakdown.
3. **Bill of Lading (sea) or Airway Bill (air)** — issued by the carrier.
4. **Certificate of Origin (CoO)** — general CoO from China Chamber of Commerce, or preferential CoO (Form A, RCEP, FTA) only when the goods demonstrably qualify under the rules of origin; eligibility is never assumed.
5. **Fiber-Lot / Compliance Certificate** — OEKO-TEX, GOTS, RWS, or buyer-specific. A buyer requirement, not a customs requirement.
6. **Textile Labeling Documents** — US FTC care labels and country-of-origin marking (16 CFR 303 / 16 CFR 423 / 19 CFR 134), and EU Regulation (EU) No 1007/2011 plus REACH statements.
7. **ISF 10+2 (US only)** — Importer Security Filing at least 24 hours before vessel loading. Late or missing filings can trigger penalties, holds, and delays; confirm exact exposure with your broker.

Preferential origin must be demonstrated under the destination's current rules; China-origin goods are not assumed eligible.

## Importing Cashmere to the USA — Process Overview

US entry requires correct classification, security filing where applicable, labeling, and broker-led clearance.

1. **Classify the goods.** Sweaters fall under HTSUS 6110, yarn under 5108 or 5109. The duty rate depends on the subheading, fiber composition, knit vs woven construction, and origin — there is no universal rate. **Cashmere import duty USA** is not a fixed number; confirm with CBP or a broker before the PO is signed.
2. **File ISF 10+2** at least 24 hours before vessel loading.
3. **CBP entry filing.** The broker files CBP Form 7501 with the invoice, packing list, CoO, and any preferential certificates.
4. **Duty payment.** Federal duty is on the entered value; rates change with HTSUS revisions. Section 301 may apply to certain China-origin codes under current policy.
5. **State sales tax.** California, New York, Texas, and Illinois each have their own treatment.
6. **Labeling compliance.** FTC care labels (16 CFR 423) and country-of-origin marking (19 CFR 134) are mandatory.
7. **Release and delivery.** Once CBP releases the cargo, the broker arranges cartage to the buyer's DC.

Budget duty, any applicable trade measures and taxes, brokerage, filing, handling, drayage, and possible examination separately. Ask the broker for a shipment-specific estimate.

## Importing Cashmere to the EU — Process Overview

The EU is a customs union with one set of external tariffs, but **cashmere import duty EU** depends on the TARIC code (10-digit), origin, and any preferential trade agreement. The UK has its own UK Global Tariff and is a separate customs territory post-Brexit.

1. **EORI number.** The EU buyer needs a valid EORI to act as importer of record; UK buyers need a UK EORI.
2. **TARIC classification.** Yarn typically classifies under 5108 / 5109, knitted garments under 6110. Each TARIC subheading has its own duty rate; anti-dumping, safeguard, and suspension measures can apply to specific origins.
3. **Preferential origin.** If the goods demonstrably qualify under EU GSP, RCEP, or another program applicable to the actual origin and HS code, a CoO can reduce or eliminate duty. Always check the current EU TARIC database — eligibility is never assumed for China-origin cashmere.
4. **ENS / ICS2.** For sea cargo, the ENS must be filed by the carrier before loading.
5. **SAD (Single Administrative Document).** The broker files the SAD at the first port of entry. The EU buyer pays customs duty (based on TARIC and origin) and VAT (typically 19–25%, depending on member state).
6. **Textile labeling and REACH.** Knitwear sold in the EU must comply with Regulation (EU) No 1007/2011 on textile fiber names and REACH for chemical safety. CE marking applies only where a specific product regulation requires it (for example certain PPE or children's products under relevant standards); it is not a general requirement for ordinary cashmere garments.
7. **Release and intra-EU transit.** Once cleared, the goods move freely within the EU customs union.

For EU landed cost, model applicable duty, import VAT, brokerage, ENS, port handling, and transit separately using a broker's current estimate.

## 5 Common Cost Mistakes B2B Buyers Make

1. **Treating an indicative freight rate as a quote.** Per-kg or per-container numbers are ranges. Bunker, peak season, congestion, and currency adjustments can move a sea rate substantially in a quarter. Always book a binding rate for a specific sailing.
2. **Assuming cashmere is always duty-free into the US or EU.** Old guides cite "0% US" and "4% EU"; duty depends on HS code, composition, and origin, and Section 301 or safeguard actions can change the math overnight. Look up the TARIC / HTSUS subheading before signing the PO.
3. **Skipping the fiber-lot certificate to "save time".** Usually required by the buyer's compliance program; skipping it costs the week anyway and breaks the OEKO-TEX / GOTS / RWS claim.
4. **Picking DDP without an EORI / importer number or HS confirmation.** DDP is convenient only if the factory has a real destination broker, the correct HS code, and a valid importer of record. Otherwise DDP just shifts the customs hold to the factory at higher cost.
5. **Booking LCL air instead of FCL sea on a full-container program.** Sea is usually a fraction of air per kg. Use FCL sea for the bulk plus a small air sample earlier for sales / press / launch.

## Procurement Checklist

1. Lock the Incoterm (FOB / CIF / DDP) in the contract.
2. Lock the HS code with a licensed broker for your specific subheading and origin.
3. Confirm preferential origin eligibility (RCEP, GSP, EU/UK FTA) only after verifying rules of origin.
4. Request the fiber-lot / compliance certificate at quotation.
5. Book freight with a forwarder that gives a binding rate for a specific sailing.
6. File ISF 10+2 (US) and ENS (EU) on time.
7. Confirm FTC labels (US) or REACH and fiber-label compliance (EU) before packing.
8. Plan pre-shipment inspection; add mid-production on yarn for >2,000 pcs.
9. Budget a landed-cost envelope on top of FOB as planning only — confirm with your broker.
10. Keep a copy of all documents with the shipment and in your ERP.

## FAQ

**Q1. Cheapest way to ship a full container of cashmere sweaters from China to the US?**
Sea freight FCL, with 2026 indicative planning figures around USD 1,800–3,500 per 20ft Tianjin to Long Beach / LA. Air on a bulk order is multiple times the sea rate.

**Q2. How long does cashmere shipping from China to Europe take by sea?**
30–38 days port-to-port Shanghai to Hamburg / Rotterdam, plus 2–7 days for clearance and delivery. Plan 120–135 days total from PO to a Hamburg DC.

**Q3. Do I really need an EORI number to import cashmere into the EU?**
Yes. Every EU importer of record needs an EORI; UK importers need a UK EORI. The broker cannot legally file the SAD without it.

**Q4. Is cashmere duty-free into the USA?**
Not as a universal rule. **Cashmere import duty USA** depends on the HTSUS subheading, fiber composition, construction, and origin; Section 301 may apply. Confirm the specific HTSUS code with CBP or a broker.

**Q5. What is the EU duty rate on cashmere knitwear?**
No single rate. **Cashmere import duty EU** depends on the TARIC code, origin, and any preferential trade agreement the goods actually qualify for. Confirm with the TARIC database and your broker.
