## 1. The 36-month view in one paragraph

Between January 2024 and September 2026, **raw cashmere fiber wholesale prices in Inner Mongolia fell roughly 32% from the late-2023 peak**, then stabilized in mid-2025 at approximately CNY 580–640/kg for Grade A 14.5–15.5µm dehaired fiber (FOB Ordos, combed, washed, sorted). This is roughly 70% of the 2022–2023 peak. The 2023 peak itself was driven by post-Covid herd restocking and a temporary export surge; the 2024–2025 correction is the return to fundamentals. Yarn and finished-goods prices followed raw fiber with a 3–4 month lag, and finished knitwear prices have been more stable than yarn because finished-goods inventory was lower.

Through 2026, our base case is **another 5–8% downside in raw fiber through Q2 2026, then flat to +2% for the balance of 2026 as herd sizes stabilize**. Translation: if you are buying cashmere in late 2026 or early 2027, you are buying in a buyer's market, but the easy 30% savings have already happened. Pricing in 2027 will be more about supplier selection than negotiation.

## 2. Raw fiber — what actually moves the price

The three things that move raw cashmere fiber price in Inner Mongolia, in order of impact over 2024–2026:

**1. Herd size in Alxa, Xilingol, and Ordos leagues.** The Alashan white cashmere goat population in the three main producing leagues was approximately 14 million head in 2022 and 13.4 million in 2025 per Alxa League statistics. The 2023–2024 decline reflected drought-related culling and reduced per-animal yield. **Smaller herd = lower per-animal yield = higher per-kg fiber price** during the 2022–2023 spike, and the reverse during 2024–2025 as the herd partially rebuilt.

**2. Downstream demand from Italian and Japanese spinners.** Biella (Italy) and the Aomori-ken (Japan) cashmere spinners are the price-setters for combed cashmere globally. When their order books are full (typically 6–9 months ahead), they absorb the Alxa-League top grades first, and the price premium for Grade A widens. When their order books soften, the spread between Grade A and Grade B narrows, and the overall index falls. This is what drove the late-2024 price softening.

**3. Export logistics from Tianjin port.** Cashmere fiber and yarn move through Tianjin and Shanghai. When ocean freight spikes (as in 2021–2022) and when customs hold times for textile exports increase (as in 2025 for some tariff lines), the FOB-CIF spread widens. This affects the buyer's landed cost more than the buyer's unit cost.

The 32% drop from late 2023 to mid 2025 was driven roughly equally by herd rebuilding and softer Biella order books. The plateau since mid 2025 is the natural floor — below this, marginal herds exit the business and the long-term supply base contracts. We do not expect significant further downside on raw fiber.

## 3. The raw-fiber-to-yarn-to-garment spread

This is the part that most "cashmere market report" content gets wrong. The spread between raw fiber and finished knitwear is not fixed — it depends on (a) machine type, (b) yarn count, (c) gauge, and (d) decoration. Here is a working breakdown based on our own 2026 transaction data for 100% cashmere products, FOB Tianjin:

| Product type | Raw fiber cost/kg finished | Processing + margin | Wholesale FOB Tianjin |

| 2/26 Nm worsted yarn, dye-lot, coned | ~$80 | +$80–110 | $165–195/kg |
| 2/48 Nm worsted yarn, dye-lot | ~$95 | +$95–125 | $190–220/kg |
| 12gg women's cashmere sweater, undyed body | ~$22 | +$15–22 | $37–44/pc |
| 12gg women's cashmere sweater, full fashion knit, dyed, link | ~$28 | +$22–32 | $50–60/pc |
| 14gg men's cashmere cardigan, link, intarsia | ~$30 | +$28–40 | $58–70/pc |
| Cashmere baby blanket 80×80 cm, woven, 2-ply | ~$28 | +$25–35 | $53–63/pc |

The right-most column is what you would see on a wholesale invoice in Q3 2026. None of these are 2023 prices; all reflect the 2024–2025 correction plus a small Q3 2026 uptick on worsted counts (driven by Biella order book re-tightening).

The takeaway for buyers: **the spread is widest on worsted dyed yarn and narrowest on commodity knitwear**. If you have flexibility on yarn construction, you have more pricing power. If you are locked into 2/48 Nm worsted in 8 specific Pantone colors, you are paying for that flexibility loss.

## 5. What 2027 looks like (our base case, October 2026)

Three scenarios, with the assumptions that move them:

**Base case (60% probability): Raw fiber stable through Q1 2027, then +3–6% in H2 2027** as Biella order book re-tightens and a smaller-than-expected 2026 spring combing yield (the goats had a dry spring in Alxa). Yarn +2–4%, finished knitwear +1–3%. This is the boring outcome, and boring is the most likely.

**Upside case (25%): +8–12% across the board by Q3 2027** if 2026 winter is severe in Inner Mongolia (heavy snow and cold = more down on the goats in March-April kidding = smaller 2027 spring yield) and if EU luxury demand continues to grow at the 2025 pace. This is the worry scenario for buyers.

**Downside case (15%): -5 to -8% across the board** if a major European luxury house continues to de-stock through 2027 and pulls down demand for top-grade fiber. Italian spinning is concentrated, and a single major customer pulling orders moves the index.

For the next 12 months, we recommend that buyers:
- Lock pricing on Q1–Q2 2027 worsted yarn orders before mid-November 2026, when the index historically firms
- Hold cashmere blanket orders into Q1 2027 — the throws index has the most upside in a cold-winter scenario
- Do not over-commit on 2/60 Nm or finer counts unless your design demands it — supply is structurally tight through 2027

## 6. How to read a cashmere supplier's quote in 2026

Three things that indicate the quote is suspect (regardless of whether it is too high or too low):

**If the quote is more than 25% below the table values in section 4**, the supplier is either (a) using Grade B or ungraded fiber marketed as Grade A, (b) blending with a non-disclosed fiber (often 20–30% wool or recycled cashmere), or (c) cutting a process step (typically the combing pass, which is the most expensive step in the fiber preparation). The first two are quality issues; the third is durability.

**If the quote is uniform across all gauges and yarn counts**, the supplier is not actually costing the order. Real cost differs by gauge, by yarn count, by stitch density, and by finishing. A 14gg men's cardigan in 2/48 Nm and a 7gg chunky blanket use different raw fiber volumes per piece and require different machine setups. A uniform quote across categories is a sign of a broker, not a producer.

**If the quote does not break out the fiber, processing, and margin components**, the buyer is being asked to negotiate blind. Our standard wholesale quote breaks these out — fiber cost, processing cost, overhead, margin — and so should yours. If a supplier refuses to break out the components, walk away.

## 7. What we are seeing in our own order book

Through Q3 2026, our order book is roughly:
- **Stable to slightly up** in 100% cashmere knitwear (sweaters, scarves), driven by mid-size European brands restocking after the 2024 destocking
- **Up materially** in cashmere throws and blankets, driven by a category that was previously a 3% of cashmere demand and is now closer to 7–8% (we attribute this to the home-textile boom and to the post-pandemic shift to home as a luxury category)
- **Up materially** in cashmere yarn (worsted counts), driven by smaller Western European brands that moved to direct-from-mill sourcing during 2024 and are now scaling
- **Down** in 100% cashmere suiting fabric, which has been quietly declining for 5 years as the worsted suiting category shifts to wool-cashmere blends

These are not universal industry trends — they are what we see from our specific customer mix. But the directional pattern is consistent with what other Ordos producers are reporting in informal conversations. Treat as a data point, not a forecast.
