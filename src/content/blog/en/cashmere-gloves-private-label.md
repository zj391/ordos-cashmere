# Private Label Cashmere Gloves: B2B Manufacturing Guide

Cashmere gloves are a high-margin private label category, with retail prices of $80–250 per pair and gift set bundles reaching $300–500. They occupy a unique position in cashmere apparel — small enough for individual gifting, luxurious enough for premium positioning, and versatile enough to work across men's and women's lines.

This guide covers manufacturing specifications, lining options, embroidery, MOQ, and target customer segments from a 23-year Ordos cashmere factory.

## Why Cashmere Gloves Are a Strong Private Label Category

Three reasons B2B buyers should consider cashmere gloves:

**High perceived value**: Gloves are a small-ticket item compared to sweaters and coats, but cashmere gloves feel like a significant gift. Retail markup of 4–6x is standard.

**Year-round demand**: Cashmere gloves work for fall, winter, and early spring. They also work indoors for cold offices and travel.

**Cross-gender appeal**: A well-designed cashmere glove in neutral colors appeals to men, women, and unisex buyers. Easy to expand into men's lines without redesigning.

## Gauge and Hand-Feel

**12 gauge**: Standard cashmere glove construction. Soft, drapey, warm. Suitable for everyday cold-weather wear.

**14 gauge**: Finer gauge produces a more refined, dressier glove. Slightly lighter. More expensive per piece but feels more premium.

**16 gauge**: Ultra-fine gauge for the most refined cashmere glove. Very lightweight (35–45g per pair). Suitable for indoor and mild-winter wear. Most expensive per piece.

Higher gauge = more knitting time = higher cost. 14 gauge adds 15–25% to per-piece price; 16 gauge adds 30–50%.

## Lining Options

**Unlined**: Standard cashmere glove. Soft against skin but limited insulation for very cold climates.

**Cashmere-lined**: Double-layer construction with cashmere interior. Warmest option. Adds USD 3–6 per pair but premium retail pricing.

**Merino-lined**: Wool interior with cashmere exterior. Warm and durable. Adds USD 2–4 per pair. Most popular for cold-climate markets.

**Silk-lined**: Silk interior with cashmere exterior. Refined, dressy, suitable for formal wear. Adds USD 2–5 per pair.

**Cashmere-silk blend lined**: Mid-price option that combines the warmth of cashmere with the smoothness of silk. Adds USD 3–5 per pair.

## Embroidery and Customization

**Embroidered logo on cuff**: Most common customization. From 100 pairs. Embroidery preserves the cashmere hand-feel better than printed labels.

**Embroidered initials on back of hand**: Premium customization for monogram programs. From 200 pairs. Adds USD 1–3 per pair.

**Custom Pantone**: From 5 kg per color. Lab dip in 5–7 days, bulk production 25–30 days after approval.

**Custom buttons or clasps**: For gauntlet-style or convertible mittens. From 200 pairs. Adds USD 2–5 per pair.

**Custom gift box**: Branded rigid box with tissue, ribbon, and care card. From 500 pairs. Adds USD 3–6 per set.

## Target Customer Segments

**Premium gift retailers**: Cashmere gloves as standalone luxury gifts, often bundled in sets with cashmere scarves or hats. MOQ 100–500 pairs per SKU.

**Department stores**: Department store private label programs. MOQ 500–2,000 pairs per SKU. Custom packaging and labeling.

**Men's specialty stores**: Refined cashmere gloves in classic colors (black, navy, charcoal, brown). MOQ 100–500 pairs per SKU. Often sold with matching cashmere scarf.

**Outdoor and travel brands**: Cashmere-lined or merino-lined cashmere gloves for cold-weather travel. MOQ 200–500 pairs per SKU. Focus on warmth and durability.

**Corporate gifting**: Branded cashmere gloves for executive gifts. MOQ 100–500 pairs per event. Custom packaging with brand logo.

## Common Mistakes B2B Buyers Make

**Mistake 1: Selling cashmere gloves as workwear.** Cashmere gloves are not designed for heavy outdoor use. Marketing them as "ski gloves" or "work gloves" creates unrealistic expectations. Position as luxury everyday gloves.

**Mistake 2: Not specifying gauge in the inquiry.** Cashmere factories can produce 12, 14, or 16 gauge construction. Gauge significantly affects hand-feel and price. Always specify.

**Mistake 3: Skipping the ribbed cuff.** A ribbed cuff holds the glove in place without elastic. Plain knit cuffs slip and require constant adjustment. Worth the USD 0.50–1.00 per pair upgrade.

**Mistake 4: Not testing lining for cold climates.** A cashmere glove without proper lining is suitable for mild winters only. For Nordic/Russian/Canadian markets, specify cashmere-lined or merino-lined construction.

**Mistake 5: Underestimating packaging costs.** Premium cashmere gloves sold as gifts require premium packaging. Rigid gift boxes add USD 3–6 per set — significant for a $80 retail product.
