# Wholesale Italian Cashmere: How to Source from Ordos at Italian Quality

Italian cashmere — the kind used by Loro Piana, Brunello Cucinelli, and Kiton — commands prices 3-5x higher than equivalent-grade Chinese cashmere. But here's what most B2B buyers don't realize: **the raw fiber in both cases comes from the same goats in Inner Mongolia and Mongolia**.

The Italian premium comes from:
1. **Italian spinning** (particularly Biella, the Italian textile valley)
2. **Italian finishing** (dye chemistry, water-softening, pressing)
3. **Italian brand markup**

If you can source the same Grade A fiber, send it to Biella for spinning, and apply Italian finishing standards, you get 90% of the Italian quality at 40-60% of the cost.

This guide explains the practical pathway.

## The Italian cashmere supply chain — deconstructed

The "Made in Italy" cashmere label hides this supply chain:

1. **Raw fiber**: Alashan or Arbas goats in Inner Mongolia (or Mongolia proper)
2. **Dehairing**: Done in China (most cost-efficient)
3. **Spinning**: Often Biella, Italy (Loro Piana, Cariaggi, Zegna Baruffa)
4. **Dyeing/finishing**: Biella or Prato
5. **Garment making**: Italy or Romania/Tunisia
6. **Brand markup**: 300-500%

The Chinese price premium for "Italian cashmere" is mostly the cost of steps 3-5 plus the brand name — not the fiber.

## How to get 90% of Italian quality from China

### Step 1: Source the same Grade A fiber

Inner Mongolia and Mongolia produce the world's best cashmere fiber. Top mills in Biella (Loro Piana, Cariaggi) source their raw fiber from the same Alashan and Arbas goats that Chinese mills use.

What's different: **selection and sorting**. Italian mills hand-sort fiber more rigorously, removing guard hair more thoroughly.

### Step 2: Send to Biella for spinning (optional but premium)

Italian spinning produces:
- **Tighter twist** (smoother yarn, less pilling)
- **More even count** (better fabric hand-feel)
- **Better dye uptake** (Italian dye chemistry is advanced)

If your customer requires "Italian yarn" branding, ship your dehaired fiber to Biella for spinning. Cost: $30-50/kg extra vs Chinese spinning.

### Step 3: Apply Italian finishing standards

The "soft hand-feel" of Italian cashmere comes from specific finishing processes:
- **Softening wash** with proprietary enzyme blends
- **Low-temperature drying** (preserves fiber integrity)
- **Steam pressing** with controlled humidity
- **Hand brushing** for surface fluffiness

These are reproducible in China with the right chemistry and equipment. Cost: ~$2-4/piece.

## When to genuinely buy "Italian cashmere" wholesale

Despite the savings, there are times when Italian cashmere makes sense:

- **Your customer demands "Made in Italy"** for legal/labeling reasons
- **You're serving the European luxury market** where Italian origin matters
- **You need Italian certification** (e.g. for textile authenticity claims)
- **You lack Chinese supplier relationships** and prefer the established Italian trade

If your end customer is price-sensitive (or cares more about quality than origin), Chinese sourcing at Italian quality levels is the rational choice.

## How to evaluate "Italian cashmere" from a Chinese supplier

When a Chinese supplier claims "Italian quality" or "Loro Piana equivalent":

1. **Ask for fiber origin documentation** — fiber source, dehairing process, micron test reports
2. **Check the spinning equipment** — STOLL/Shima Seiki vs cheap Chinese machines
3. **Request comparison swatches** against an actual Italian garment
4. **Verify finishing chemistry** — OEKO-TEX Class I certification, ZDHC compliance
5. **Test pilling resistance** — Italian cashmere pills less because of softer spinning. Test with Martindale abrasion.

A genuine premium Chinese mill will provide all 5 without hesitation.

## Practical path: hybrid sourcing

For most B2B buyers, the optimal strategy is **hybrid sourcing**:

- **Premium capsule collections**: Italian-spun yarn, Chinese-made garments, "Made in China" labels acceptable
- **Volume production**: 100% Chinese supply chain with Italian-level finishing
- **European luxury contracts**: 100% Italian supply chain for label compliance

This gives you flexibility to optimize for cost, lead time, and market requirements.
