# Cashmere vs Merino Wool for Wholesale: Which Fiber Suits Your Product Line?

When planning a knitwear or accessory line, choosing between cashmere and merino wool is a foundational decision that affects every other choice — pricing, target market, design, durability expectations.

This guide compares the two fibers on the dimensions that matter for B2B buyers: cost, hand-feel, durability, care requirements, and market positioning.

## Side-by-side comparison

| Property | Cashmere | Merino Wool |
|---|---|---|
| Micron count | 14.0-19.0μm (luxury: 14-15.5μm) | 11.5-24μm (luxury: 11.5-18μm) |
| Fiber length | 28-42mm | 50-120mm |
| Softness | Softer (smaller diameter) | Soft, but more "scratch" potential at >19μm |
| Warmth per gram | 3x warmer than wool | Warm |
| Weight per sweater (M size) | 280-400g | 380-550g |
| Cost (Grade A fiber per kg) | $75-120 | $8-25 |
| Garment FOB cost (M sweater) | $45-90 | $18-45 |
| Pilling resistance | Lower (shorter fiber) | Higher (longer fiber) |
| Care | Hand wash or dry clean | Machine washable (some) |
| Lifespan | 5-10 years with care | 3-7 years |
| Origin | Inner Mongolia, Mongolia | Australia, New Zealand, South Africa |
| Sustainability | Lower (smaller yield per goat) | Higher (larger flocks) |

## When to choose cashmere

Cashmere is the right choice when:

- **Price point**: USD 80-300+ retail (luxury positioning)
- **Customer**: Premium fashion brands, gift market, luxury buyers
- **Product**: Lightweight sweaters, scarves, accessories, layering pieces
- **Marketing angle**: Softness, warmth, exclusivity, prestige
- **Volume**: Lower volume, higher margin (e.g. 1,000 pieces at $150 retail)

Cashmere excels at warmth-to-weight ratio, which is why it's preferred for:
- Travel clothing (light, warm, packs small)
- Lingerie-adjacent garments (next-to-skin softness)
- Luxury accessories (scarves, hats, gloves)

## When to choose merino

Merino is the right choice when:

- **Price point**: USD 30-150 retail (mid-tier to premium)
- **Customer**: Outdoor enthusiasts, everyday wear, performance market
- **Product**: Heavy sweaters, base layers, socks, performance wear
- **Marketing angle**: Performance, durability, easy care, sustainability
- **Volume**: Higher volume, lower margin (e.g. 5,000+ pieces)

Merino excels at:
- Durability and pilling resistance
- Machine washability (most merino)
- Moisture wicking (for base layers and activewear)
- Sustainability story (wool is renewable, biodegradable)

## Cashmere-merino blends: a middle path

Many brands blend the two to balance cost, hand-feel, and durability:

| Blend | Hand-feel | Cost | Durability | Use case |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 100% cashmere | Softest | $$$ | Low | Luxury |
| 70% cashmere / 30% merino | Soft | $$$ | Medium | Premium without 100% cashmere price |
| 50/50 | Soft-medium | $$ | Medium | Mid-tier premium |
| 30% cashmere / 70% merino | Medium | $$ | Higher | Soft touch at lower price |
| 100% merino | Less soft | $ | High | Everyday wear |

For wholesale buyers, **the 70/30 cashmere/merino blend is often the sweet spot** — close to cashmere hand-feel, 30-40% cheaper, and noticeably more durable.

## Market positioning in 2026

The wholesale market has shifted:

- **2015**: Cashmere was 3-5x more expensive than merino
- **2020**: Cashmere was 2-3x more expensive
- **2026**: Cashmere (Grade B/C) is 1.5-2x more expensive than premium merino

Why? Increased cashmere supply (more Inner Mongolian farms), better grading technology, and Chinese domestic market competition have compressed cashmere margins. Grade A cashmere is still premium-priced, but mid-grade is closer to merino than ever.

For B2B buyers, this means:
- **Buyers who used to source only cashmere** are now considering merino for everyday lines
- **Buyers who used to source only merino** are now able to add cashmere capsule collections at lower entry cost
- **Cashmere-merino blends** have become the default for mid-tier brands

## How to source each fiber

### Cashmere sourcing

- **Inner Mongolia** (Alashan, Arbas): best Grade A fiber
- **Mongolia**: premium, often used by Italian mills
- **Iran/Afghanistan**: lower grade, lower price
- **Direct from dehairing mills** is cheaper than trading companies

For B2B buyers entering cashmere, **MOQ 50-100 pieces** is now realistic (was 500+ a decade ago).

### Merino sourcing

- **Australia**: most premium merino, branded (e.g. Merino woolmark-certified)
- **New Zealand**: similar quality, smaller industry
- **South Africa**: lower cost, growing industry
- **South America**: commodity merino

For B2B buyers, **machine-washable merino** is a strong market differentiator — most cashmere is hand-wash or dry-clean only.

## Decision framework for your next product line

Ask these questions:

1. **What's your target retail price point?**
   - $30-80: merino or blend
   - $80-200: cashmere blend or Grade B cashmere
   - $200+: Grade A cashmere

2. **What's your customer's care tolerance?**
   - Wants machine washable: merino
   - Accepts hand wash: cashmere
   - Requires dry clean only: high-end cashmere

3. **What's your volume?**
   - 100-500 pieces: cashmere is viable
   - 500-5,000 pieces: cashmere-merino blend or premium merino
   - 5,000+: merino or basic cashmere

4. **What's your sustainability story?**
   - Renewable, biodegradable: merino (better)
   - Premium natural: cashmere (more exclusive)
   - Both: blend, with proper sourcing documentation

## Our recommendation for most B2B buyers

If you're launching a new premium knitwear line, **start with a 70/30 cashmere-merino blend**. It gives you 90% of cashmere's hand-feel at 50-60% of the cost, with better durability. As your brand matures and margins improve, introduce a 100% Grade A cashmere capsule collection.

For accessories (hats, scarves, gloves), 100% cashmere is the right call — these items are priced higher per gram than garments, and cashmere softness matters more for direct-to-skin wear.
