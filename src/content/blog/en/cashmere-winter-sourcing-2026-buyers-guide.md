# 2026 Cashmere Sourcing Calendar: When to Order, Book Production, and Receive Goods

As a global buyer of cashmere products—whether you’re a brand owner, importer, or trading company—timing is everything. The cashmere supply chain is uniquely seasonal, with raw material availability, manufacturing capacity, and shipping windows all influencing your ability to deliver products to market on time. This comprehensive sourcing calendar for 2026 will help you align your purchasing decisions with the industry’s natural rhythm, ensuring you secure the best quality, minimize risks, and optimize your inventory flow.

## Why a Sourcing Calendar Matters for Cashmere

Cashmere is not a commodity you can order at any time. The entire production cycle—from goat herding to finished garments—follows a strict annual schedule. Missing a critical window can mean delayed deliveries, higher costs, or even inability to secure the finest fibers. For B2B buyers, a well-planned sourcing calendar is your strategic advantage. It allows you to:

- Secure raw material at competitive prices before shortages occur.
- Book production slots with manufacturers before they fill up.
- Plan shipping to avoid peak-season congestion.
- Align your product launches with consumer demand peaks.

## Overview of the Cashmere Production Cycle

To understand the calendar, you need to grasp the stages of cashmere production:

1. **Dehairing** (raw fiber processing) – occurs in spring/summer.
2. **Dyeing and spinning** – typically summer to early autumn.
3. **Knitting/weaving** – autumn and early winter.
4. **Garment finishing** – late autumn to winter.
5. **Quality inspection and packaging** – throughout production.

Each stage has its own lead time, and they overlap depending on the product type (raw material, yarn, or finished garments).

## The 2026 Cashmere Sourcing Calendar: Month-by-Month Breakdown

Here is a month-by-month guide to help you plan your cashmere sourcing activities for 2026. Note that this calendar is based on the Northern Hemisphere (specifically Inner Mongolia, China) and typical lead times. Adjust for your specific time zone and shipping routes.

### January – March: Strategic Planning and Raw Material Assessment

- **Evaluate your 2026/2027 needs**: Determine your product lines, quantities, and target markets.
- **Monitor raw material prices**: Cashmere prices fluctuate based on supply and demand. Stay in touch with your suppliers for market updates.
- **Visit trade shows**: If possible, attend industry events like the China International Cashmere Forum (usually early in the year) to network and gather intelligence.
- **Place early raw material orders**: For those who want to secure the finest fibers (e.g., 15.5-16 microns), early commitment is essential. Some suppliers accept orders for the upcoming combing season.

**Key Action**: Finalize your product specifications and budget for the year.

### April – June: Raw Material Procurement and Pre-Production

- **April**: The dehairing season begins in Inner Mongolia. This is when the previous year’s raw cashmere is processed. If you haven’t ordered raw material yet, now is the time—but expect limited availability for premium grades.
- **May**: Yarn spinning capacity starts to become booked. For yarn orders, confirm your requirements and place orders to secure production slots.
- **June**: By the end of June, most raw material for the year is sold. If you are a yarn buyer, you should have your orders in by now to ensure delivery in Q4.

**Key Action**: For yarn and fabric orders, finalize contracts by end of May.

### July – August: Production Booking and Early Manufacturing

- **July**: This is the peak time for placing production orders for finished garments. Manufacturers are now scheduling their knitting and weaving lines. Booking early ensures you get your preferred production window.
- **August**: Production of knitwear and woven fabrics begins. If you are sourcing garments, your orders should be confirmed by August to meet holiday season deadlines.

**Key Action**: Secure production slots for all finished goods by August.

### September – October: Production and Quality Control

- **September**: Full-scale production is underway. This is also the time for quality control inspections. If you are using third-party inspection services, schedule them now.
- **October**: For orders that need to arrive before the winter sales season, production should be completed by mid-October. This allows for shipping time.

**Key Action**: Monitor production progress closely and arrange inspections.

### November – December: Shipping and Last-Minute Orders

- **November**: Most orders are shipped out. If you are in the US or Europe, ocean freight takes 3-5 weeks, so plan accordingly.
- **December**: Only emergency orders or very small quantities can be produced now. Expect higher costs and longer lead times.

**Key Action**: Ensure all shipments are booked and tracked.

**Pro Tip**: Always add a safety buffer of 2-3 weeks for unexpected delays (e.g., raw material shortages, shipping congestion).

## Seasonal Sourcing Windows: When to Order for Which Season

Cashmere is typically associated with winter, but many brands also offer transitional pieces. Here’s a quick guide:

- **For Winter (Nov-Feb)**: Order in January-March for raw material, April-June for yarn, and July-August for finished garments. Ship by September-October.
- **For Autumn (Sep-Oct)**: Order earlier—raw material in January, yarn in March-April, garments in May-June.
- **For Spring (Mar-Apr)**: Lightweight cashmere blends. Order yarn in October-November of the previous year, garments in December-January.
