## Choosing the Right Gauge for Your Retail Tier

**5gg heavy gauge**: 480–620g cardigan weight. Visibly chunky knit, drapes heavily, reads as outerwear. The right choice for FW 2026 luxury programs where the cardigan replaces a jacket. Most expensive in fiber cost.

**7gg chunky**: 380–480g cardigan weight. Visible knit texture, premium casual aesthetic. The standard for boutique cashmere cardigan programs.

**12gg refined**: 280–340g cardigan weight. Smooth surface, refined drape, office-appropriate. The most-requested gauge for B2B programs — works under a jacket or alone.

**14gg fine-gauge**: 220–280g cardigan weight. Smooth drape, dressier aesthetic. Common for office-wear programs where the cardigan substitutes for a sweater vest.

For FW 2026, the 12gg refined tier is the most-requested at our facility. The 7gg chunky tier is the fastest-growing segment, driven by the layering-trend.

## V-Depth and Placket Construction

**V-depth** (the distance from the shoulder seam to the V point):
- 18cm: modest, office-appropriate, layers well over a collared shirt
- 20cm: standard, suits most retail programs
- 22cm: deeper, more fashion-forward, suits smart-casual retail
- 24cm+: very deep, statement piece, suits editorial retail

**Placket width** (the reinforced front band on each side of the V):
- 2.5cm: refined, suits 12gg cardigans
- 3cm: standard, suits 7gg chunky
- 3.5cm: chunky, suits 5gg heavy gauge

**Button count and spacing**: 5 buttons is standard for cashmere cardigans (V-depth 20–22cm). 6 buttons for deeper V (22–26cm). 4 buttons for shorter V (16–18cm). Button spacing should be 9–10cm for refined, 10–11cm for chunky.

**Rib trim**: the V-neck rib trim is the most visible detail. Specify rib width (2–2.5cm standard), rib gauge (matching body gauge), and rib fold behavior (lay-flat or fold-over).

## Common Mistakes B2B Buyers Make

**Mistake 1: Specifying a 14gg cardigan with 5 buttons.** The placket looks disproportionate to the gauge. Match the placket width and button size to the gauge tier.

**Mistake 2: Skipping the rib-trim specification.** Cashmere rib trim stretches differently from the body knit. Specify rib width, gauge, and recovery target explicitly.

**Mistake 3: Underestimating the placket yarn consumption.** The placket, button band, and rib trim together add 30–45g of yarn per cardigan versus a pullover. Budget this for both cost and lead-time.

**Mistake 4: Ordering plastic buttons on a $250+ retail cardigan.** Button material is a visible brand-quality signal. Horn or mother-of-pearl matches cashmere; plastic breaks the brand promise.

**Mistake 5: Forgetting to specify rib stretch target.** Cardigan rib trim should recover to 90%+ of original length after 30 minutes. Specify this in the bulk contract to avoid stretch-out complaints.
