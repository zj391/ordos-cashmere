## Five Price Drivers We Are Watching in 2026

### 1. Herder-side supply and grazing quotas

Inner Mongolia continues to manage goat herds through grazing permits and grassland-restoration programs. In 2026, the supply side is **stable-to-slightly-down** versus 2024–2025, which keeps a soft floor under prices. Weather risk (a cold winter plus dry spring) is the most likely 2026 supply shock.

### 2. Dehairing capacity and yield

Most raw cashmere is sold as "raw" with 30–50% grease + guard hair; dehaired cashmere is the tradeable unit. In 2026, dehairing capacity in the Ordos and Bayannur clusters is steady, but yield per kg of raw fiber has trended down ~2–3% versus 2023 due to finer average fiber diameter. Lower yield mechanically raises the dehaired price even if the herd count is flat.

### 3. Demand from luxury and DTC channels

Luxury demand in Europe, Japan, and the US has been resilient, and DTC mid-premium brands continue to grow. This is the main reason we have not seen a 2026 price collapse despite softer macro indicators elsewhere. For more on how this flows into a finished sweater, see our [cashmere cost breakdown](/en/blog/cashmere-cost-breakdown-wholesale).

### 4. Currency, freight, and tariffs

Because cashmere is invoiced in USD to most international buyers, RMB strength raises the local-currency price. Sea-freight rates on China–EU / China–US lanes are within normal 2025 bands in mid-2026, but a single fuel shock can move the landed cost by 5–10%. Tariffs are country-specific (EU 4% duty + VAT, US HTS 6110 typically 0% on cashmere sweaters, Japan 0–7.5%) and should be modelled separately.

### 5. Certification and traceability paperwork

OEKO-TEX Standard 100, GOTS (organic), RWS (responsible wool/cashmere standard), and the new Inner Mongolia geographical indication (GI) all add cost at the lot level. In 2026 we see buyers increasingly demanding fiber-lot certificates — not just finished-goods certificates — which is a healthy trend but raises documentation time.

## Regional Supply Outlook

### China (Inner Mongolia, especially Ordos)

The dominant source. Ordos-region factories remain the most vertically integrated in the world, with raw fiber, dehairing, spinning, knitting, and finishing often under one group. For B2B buyers targeting mid-premium cashmere at scale, this is still the default choice. Our [raw material page](/en/raw-material) details the lot-by-lot sourcing model we use.

### Mongolia

Lower-cost alternative, but with thinner manufacturing depth. Mongolian raw fiber is real and abundant, but most premium yarn is still spun in China or Italy. For 2026, expect 15–25% lower FOB sweater prices versus China, offset by longer lead times and more variable yarn quality.

### Afghanistan / Iran (Khorasan)

A smaller, niche source for very long, coarse fiber (often 17–19μm), typically used in carpets, hand-loomed shawls, and rustic knitwear. Not a substitute for fine Chinese cashmere, but a complementary source for artisanal programs.

### Italy and Scotland

Buyers, not primary raw-fiber sources. Italian and Scottish mills comb and spin premium fiber (often imported from China or Mongolia) into ultra-fine yarn, then finish garments. Their added value is in spinning, finishing, and brand — not in raw fiber.

### Australia and New Zealand

Emerging farm-raised cashmere. Volumes are still small, but traceability and animal-welfare certifications are strong. Expect to pay a 20–40% premium versus equivalent Chinese fiber, in exchange for lot-level provenance.

## Procurement Checklist (for 2026 sourcing)

1. **Define micron and length, not just "cashmere".** Specify 14.5μm / 36mm, or 15.5μm / 34mm. "Cashmere" alone invites the cheapest grade that fits your label.
2. **Ask for the fiber-lot certificate, not just the finished-goods certificate.** OEKO-TEX, GOTS, and GI documentation should accompany the dehaired fiber invoice.
3. **Lock yarn 60–90 days before cut.** Yarn spot markets exist but pricing is volatile in Q3–Q4.
4. **Negotiate on volume and gauge, not on fiber grade.** Pushing the factory to "drop the fiber price" almost always means downgraded grade.
5. **Budget 12% EU duty / 0% US duty on cashmere sweaters** (HS 6110) as a starting point; verify with your broker.
6. **Plan two inspection points:** pre-shipment on finished goods, plus an optional mid-production yarn inspection for orders over 2,000 pcs.
