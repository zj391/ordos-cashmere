# 2026-2027 Cashmere Color Forecasting: 5 Pantone-Inspired Palettes for FW26 / SS27

Every 12 months we run a color forecasting exercise with the dye-house team in our Ordos facility, looking at the Pantone Fashion Color Report, the Italian Biella spinning-mill color stories, and what is in the sample room from our wholesale clients. This is the FW26 / SS27 summary — 5 palettes, the Pantone references, and what each one means in cashmere terms. These are not predictions of what will sell; they are the palettes we are actively running dye-lots on for FW26 production and SS27 sampling.

If you are a brand building your FW26 capsule or SS27 collection, treat this as a working reference. If you are a yarn buyer selecting stock for a 2027 program, the palettes at the back of this page are the ones to dye-lot now.

## 1. How to read a cashmere color forecast

Cashmere is a finer fiber than wool, which means it accepts dye differently in three important ways:

**Deeper, more saturated absorption.** Cashmere's smaller fiber diameter means more cuticle surface per gram, which means dyes bond to a higher proportion of fiber surface. A color that reads as "dusted rose" on merino will read as "rose" on cashmere. Pantone reference numbers are useful as a starting point, but cashmere tends to render one to two steps deeper than wool.

**More warmth in the dye bath, less of a "cool" cast.** This is a property of the fiber's natural pigment (cashmere is slightly yellower than wool at the same grade). Cool grays on cashmere tend to pull warm. Blue-grays (Pantone 18-3949) need extra shading correction in the dye bath. This is not a problem, it is just something to know in advance.

**More variation between dye lots in undyed natural shades.** Undyed natural cashmere varies in shade from lot to lot because the goats' undercoat color varies seasonally. If you want a strict "natural" color across multiple seasons, you need a custom blending program. We do this for our larger clients, with a 3% shade tolerance documented in the spec.

These three properties shape everything below. When we say "this palette is robust," we mean the colors render consistently across lots and mills. When we say "this palette requires a custom blending program," we mean the buyer should plan for an extra step.

## 2. Palette I — "Eclipse Earth" (FW26 lead palette)

Anchored on a deep, grounded brown family. This is the dominant direction we are seeing in Italian cashmere for FW26 — warm umbers, mushroom, deep cocoa, charcoal with warmth.

| Color | Pantone reference | Cashmere equivalent | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Eclipse Cocoa | 19-0915 | "Truffle" — deep cool brown | Top-seller direction for FW26 across Italian and Japanese buyers |
| Charred Umber | 19-1020 | "Bistre" — warm deep brown | Pairs well with cream and rust accents |
| Driftwood | 14-1023 | "Driftwood" — cool mushroom | Works as the "neutral" in this palette, not warm beige |
| Carbon Mist | 19-0303 | "Soot" — warm near-black | Reads warmer than Pantone; request the dye-lot before signing off |
| Bone Linen | 11-0602 | "Linen" — warm cream | The accent; pairs especially well with the darker four |

**What it does to yarn price:** Neutral, low-risk. The browns and mushroom dyes are in our standing dye-lot program and run at no premium. Charcoal with warm cast (Carbon Mist) is slightly more expensive because the dye recipe needs extra shading correction — roughly 3–5% premium over a true neutral grey.

**Who it works for:** Heritage and contemporary heritage menswear, transitional outerwear, premium scarf programs, and home textiles. This is the safest FW26 palette for any brand not willing to take a color risk.

## 3. Palette II — "Botanica" (FW26 accent / SS27 lead)

Greens, soft yellows, mushroom — botanical and a little bit pre-Raphaelite. This is the palette that has been building since the late-2024 quiet-luxury trend. It is significantly different from Eclipse Earth and signals a more feminine, more "soft luxury" direction.

| Color | Pantone reference | Cashmere equivalent | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Botanical Sage | 15-0332 | "Sage" — desaturated leaf green | The hero color of this palette |
| Pampas | 13-1008 | "Pampas" — warm sandy beige | Sits between brown and yellow, very cashmere-friendly |
| Soft Ochre | 14-0848 | "Soft Ochre" — warm muted yellow | Works as a "faded gold" accent |
| Ivory Mint | 11-0907 | "Ivory Mint" — pale green cream | Tricky in cashmere — request a dye-lot, the green can shift |
| Tender Stem | 16-0220 | "Sage" derivative — slightly more grey | Pairs with the rest of the palette for layering |

**What it does to yarn price:** Moderate premium, especially for Botanical Sage. Sage greens require careful dye bath balancing to avoid shifting too cool or too warm. We dye-lot sage in 25kg minimums; below that, the unit cost is 6–8% higher.

**Who it works for:** Womenswear capsules, premium home textiles, and any FW26 program that wants a softer, more "natural" alternative to a pure neutral story. Sage has been building for two seasons and is now safe for mainstream use.

## 4. Palette III — "Twilight Plum" (FW26 evening / SS27 transition)

Deep purples, ink blues, and one precise "twilight" mauve. This is the high-fashion direction for FW26 — evening wear, premium scarves, and any program with a more editorial positioning.

**What it does to yarn price:** Higher premium, especially for Ink Plum. The purple dyes have shorter bath life and require extra rinse cycles, which adds 4–6% to the dye-lot cost. Mid-tier factories often can't hit the deep-plum tone that high-end cashmere can; the difference is the dye recipe and the post-dye washing, which is where we spend extra.

**Who it works for:** Premium womenswear, evening and occasion knitwear, statement scarves, and gift programs. This is the highest-priced palette in the forecast — Ink Plum dyed cashmere is currently a 12–15% premium over comparable neutrals.

## 7. The cross-palette program — how to combine

For a complete FW26 capsule (60–80 pieces across knitwear and accessories), the safest editorial combination is:

- **60–70% in Eclipse Earth (Palette I)** — Eclipse Cocoa, Driftwood, Bone Linen
- **15–20% in Botanica (Palette II)** — Botanical Sage, Pampas
- **8–12% in Twilight Plum (Palette III)** — Ink Plum and Storm Black for evening pieces
- **5–8% in Saffron Caramel (Palette V)** — one or two hero pieces

This gives you a 4-color story with enough range for both men's and women's, both knitwear and scarves, both apparel and home textiles, without taking a risk that the program will not have a saleable core.

For SS27, shift the balance toward Boreal (Palette IV) — 50% Boreal, 30% Eclipse Earth, 20% Botanica accents. Boreal is the future-neutral; Eclipse Earth is the bridge season.

## 9. What we are not doing in 2026

Two directions we are deliberately not running:

**Metallic / lurex blends.** We had a few requests in early 2026. Cashmere is a luxury fiber precisely because it is not shiny. Adding metallic contradicts the material's value proposition. We decline these programs.

**Bright neons.** The cashmere fiber structure absorbs neon dye unevenly. The result is a flat neon, which is not what a buyer wants when they specify neon. The market for cashmere neons is essentially zero, and we do not recommend it.

If a designer asks for either of these, we will redirect to a "softer" version in Palette V — Saffron Caramel for the wow, Apricot Glow for the playful. The customer usually agrees once they see the cashmere rendering.

Custom colors are common in cashmere. The flow is:

1. Send Pantone reference + your target lighting (warm, neutral, cool) + the garment type + the gauge + the MOQ.
