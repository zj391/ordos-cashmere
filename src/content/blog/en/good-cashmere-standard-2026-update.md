## What the Good Cashmere Standard is, and who maintains it

The Good Cashmere Standard (GCS) is an independent standard for cashmere fibre, set and administered by the Aid by Trade Foundation (AbTF). The standard defines animal welfare, environmental, and chain-of-custody criteria for raw cashmere, and provides a labelled pathway for downstream products. The AbTF publishes the standard documents and the list of certified farms, dehairing facilities and brands. [1](https://www.goodcashmerestandard.com/)

In 2026, the AbTF released an updated version of the Good Cashmere Standard with refined animal welfare criteria and expanded traceability requirements for the dehairing and trading stages. The updates apply to all newly certified operations from 1 January 2026; existing certified operations transition during 2026 in line with the AbTF's published timetable. [1](https://www.goodcashmerestandard.com/)

#### Where to confirm the current version

The most reliable reference for the standard's current text, the transition timetable and the list of certified operations is the AbTF's own publication. When confirming compliance or preparing an inquiry, treat the AbTF site as the canonical source rather than secondary summaries. [1](https://www.goodcashmerestandard.com/)

#### Practical areas updated for 2026

Based on the AbTF's published updates, three areas are most relevant to a cashmere B2B buyer:

1. **Animal welfare criteria** — the standard already covers grazing, shelter, handling and shearing. The 2026 update raises expectations on cold-weather shelter, the timing and method of fibre collection, and the documentation of veterinary care. The objective is to align GCS with broader animal welfare standard practice without changing the underlying principle of combing rather than shearing. [1](https://www.goodcashmerestandard.com/)

2. **Chain-of-custody documentation** — the updated standard expands the requirement to record fibre origin, herd type, and the dehairing facility for each lot traded under the standard. The records must be auditable; the AbTF publishes the audit frequency and the certification body's scope. [1](https://www.goodcashmerestandard.com/)

3. **Brand and retailer obligations** — brands and retailers using the GCS label must keep the labelled fibre traceable through processing stages. This is the practical impact on a private-label or retail program sourcing GCS-aligned cashmere. [1](https://www.goodcashmerestandard.com/)

These are administrative controls, not a guarantee of any specific outcome. The actual scope, evidence and certification body decision depend on the AbTF's published rules and the buyer's product category.

#### What a B2B buyer can confirm with a cashmere supplier

For a private-label, retail, or wholesale program using GCS-aligned cashmere, the following records are typically worth confirming in writing:

| Area | Practical record to ask for | Why it is useful now |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Certification status | GCS certificate number and scope, valid until date | Confirms the operation is currently certified |
| Origin | Region, herd type, list of certified farms | Supports fibre origin claims and DPP-style records |
| Dehairing | Certified dehairing facility name and address | Demonstrates chain-of-custody compliance |
| Lot reference | Internal lot, bale or shipment reference linked to origin records | Allows traceability per shipment |
| Brand licence | Brand or retailer's own GCS label licence number, if applicable | Confirms the downstream user is licensed to use the mark |
| Documentation owner | Which party is responsible for each record | Clarifies the data request process |

The actual scope depends on the buyer's product, destination and brand policy. Treat this list as a starting point for an inquiry, not as a standard checklist.

#### How GCS relates to other cashmere frameworks

GCS is one of several frameworks a cashmere buyer may need to evaluate together. Each framework has its own scope:

- **Textile Exchange Recycled Cashmere Standard** — for cashmere fibre recovered from pre- or post-consumer textile waste.
- **OEKO-TEX Standard 100** — for finished textile products at the consumer level.
- **EU CSDDD / DPP** — chain-of-custody records for the largest companies selling textile products in the EU.

A cashmere supplier may participate in one or several frameworks. The buyer should clarify which framework applies to each material or product, rather than assume a single certification covers everything.
