The global fashion industry is under increasing pressure to adopt circular practices, and cashmere—a luxury fiber with a significant environmental footprint—is no exception. ## Understanding Recycled Cashmere: Definitions and Sources

Recycled cashmere is produced by breaking down discarded cashmere garments or production offcuts into fiber, which is then spun into new yarn. In the B2B context, there are two primary sources:

- **Pre-consumer waste**: Offcuts, clippings, and defective knitwear from manufacturing processes. This is the most common and controllable source.
- **Post-consumer waste**: Garments collected from consumers, which require more intensive sorting and cleaning.

For most B2B brands, pre-consumer recycled cashmere is the preferred choice because it offers consistent quality and traceability. At DONGXIAO, we source pre-consumer waste from our own production lines and certified suppliers, ensuring a closed-loop system that minimizes contamination.

## The Quality Conundrum: Does Recycling Compromise Cashmere's Luxury?

One of the biggest fears among buyers is that recycled cashmere will feel harsh or pill excessively. This concern is valid if the recycling process is poorly managed. However, with modern technology and strict quality control, recycled cashmere can achieve remarkable quality. Here’s how:

### Fiber Length and Strength

The key to quality is fiber length. During recycling, fibers are mechanically shortened. Virgin cashmere fibers typically range from 30 to 40 mm, while recycled fibers may be 20 to 30 mm. To maintain strength, we blend recycled fibers with a percentage of virgin cashmere or other natural fibers. A common blend is 70% recycled cashmere and 30% virgin cashmere, which yields a yarn that meets most commercial standards.

### Micron Count and Softness

Micron count (fiber diameter) determines softness. Premium virgin cashmere is around 15.5–16.5 microns. Recycled fibers, because they are shorter, can feel slightly different, but by using fine-quality pre-consumer waste, we can retain a micron count of 17–18.5 microns, which is still considered luxurious. Our factory in Ordos, Inner Mongolia, has access to some of the finest cashmere in the world, ensuring that even our recycled lines maintain a premium feel.

### Pilling and Durability

Pilling is a common issue with recycled cashmere if the yarn is not properly twisted. By adjusting twist levels and using anti-pilling finishes, we can produce recycled cashmere yarn that performs comparably to virgin yarn. In fact, our recycled yarns have passed rigorous Martindale abrasion tests, with results within industry norms.

## The Environmental and Economic Case for Recycled Cashmere

The environmental benefits are compelling. Producing 1 kg of virgin cashmere requires the annual fleece of about 3-4 goats, and overgrazing in Inner Mongolia has led to desertification concerns. Recycling cashmere reduces the demand for new fiber, saving water, energy, and land. According to a life-cycle assessment, recycled cashmere can reduce carbon emissions by up to 80% compared to virgin production.

Economically, recycled cashmere is typically 20–30% cheaper than virgin cashmere, making it an attractive option for brands targeting mid-market segments or seeking to offset higher prices of sustainable certifications. However, the cost savings depend on the blend ratio and processing complexity.

## Sourcing and Certifications: What to Look For

When sourcing recycled cashmere, ensure your supplier provides:

- **Traceability**: Documentation showing the origin of pre-consumer waste.
- **Certifications**: Look for OEKO-TEX, GRS (Global Recycled Standard), or GOTS. GRS is particularly relevant for recycled content.
- **Test Reports**: Independent lab reports verifying fiber composition and quality parameters.
