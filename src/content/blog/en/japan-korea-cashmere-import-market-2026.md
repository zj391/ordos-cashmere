## What we observed at a glance

Japan and South Korea are the largest Asian cashmere knitwear import markets outside Greater China. The two markets differ in buyer profile, product preference and import flow, but share several practical concerns: premium grade selection, lot consistency, documentation requirements and brand-led marketing claims. This note covers both markets for comparison; specific buyer assumptions should be confirmed per inquiry.

The numbers cited below are working estimates drawn from publicly available trade statistics and the patterns observed at our facility. Where a figure is precise, the source is named; where it is directional, it is described as such.

#### Japan — premium grade, brand-led, low MOQ per style

The Japan cashmere knitwear import market is dominated by:

- **Luxury houses** (e.g. the established Japanese luxury conglomerates and the European houses with Japanese subsidiaries) sourcing premium-grade yarn for in-house knitwear programs.
- **Mid-tier Japanese brands** that produce seasonal knitwear for the domestic department store and select-shop retail channels.
- **Boutique brands and ateliers** that work with small lots and prioritise grade, hand-feel and pattern development.

Key practical considerations for Japan-bound cashmere programs:

- **Grade preference**: Japanese buyers typically specify fine micron ranges (often 14–15.5 μm, sometimes finer) and request grade documentation per lot.
- **Lot consistency**: Programs tend to favour suppliers that can deliver consistent micron range, length and down content across multiple deliveries within a season.
- **Ply and construction**: The Japan market has a strong preference for 2-ply and 12-gauge knitwear, with seasonal variation between thicker gauge for autumn/winter and finer gauge for spring layering.
- **Yarn twist and hand-feel**: Japanese knitters and brand product teams are typically hand-feel-conscious. Yarn sample swatches are often requested before bulk order confirmation.
- **Documentation**: Beyond the standard invoice and packing list, Japanese buyers may request certificate of origin, fibre declaration and, for some programs, an explicit "Mongolian cashmere" origin statement.
- **Lead time and seasonality**: Programs are typically planned 6–9 months ahead, with autumn/winter deliveries peaking in July–September for arrival in October–November.

#### South Korea — growth segment, K-fashion-led, direct sourcing

The Korean cashmere knitwear import market has expanded as K-fashion brands have grown globally. The buyer landscape includes:

- **Mid- to high-end Korean brands** sourcing directly from Inner Mongolia for premium programs.
- **Cashmere specialists and ODM factories** in Korea working with both Korean and Japanese buyers.
- **Online-first brands** that market cashmere knitwear as a premium category, often with a transparent pricing and origin narrative.

Key practical considerations for Korea-bound cashmere programs:

- **Direct sourcing**: Korean brands are more likely to source directly from the mill or the dehairing facility, rather than through a trading house, in line with the trend toward transparent origin claims.
- **Lot traceability**: Several Korean brands ask for per-lot fibre origin records, including farm region and dehairing facility, in line with the broader traceability trend.
- **Yarn selection**: Korean buyers tend to specify 14–15.5 μm micron range for premium programs; some also work with 15.5–16.5 μm for entry-level lines.
- **Lead time**: Korean programs tend to be planned 4–6 months ahead, slightly shorter than Japan, with greater willingness to sample across multiple options.
- **Documentation**: Korean buyers typically request certificate of origin and fibre declaration; some also request specific labelling language for the consumer-facing tag.

#### Common practical records for both markets

The following records are typically worth confirming in writing for any Japan- or Korea-bound cashmere program, regardless of the specific buyer:

| Area | Practical record | Why it is useful now |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Origin | Region (Ordos, Alashan, other Inner Mongolia sub-region) and herd type | Supports premium positioning and brand claims |
| Fibre grade | Micron range, length, down percentage | Supports hand-feel and quality claims |
| Lot reference | Lot or shipment reference linked to origin | Supports traceability per delivery |
| Composition | Per-style fibre declaration, including any blend components | Supports labelling and import declaration |
| Care guidance | Approved care label wording in the destination language | Supports product use and warranty claims |
| Documentation owner | Which party is responsible for each record | Clarifies the data request process |

These are administrative controls, not guarantees of any specific outcome. The actual scope, evidence and label wording depend on the buyer's brand policy and the destination's regulatory requirements.
