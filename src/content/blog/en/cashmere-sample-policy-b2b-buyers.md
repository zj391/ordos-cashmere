# Cashmere Sample Policy for B2B Buyers: Costs, Lead Times, Refunds, and How to Read a Factory's Sample Terms (2026)

For B2B buyers evaluating a new cashmere supplier, the **sample order** is the highest-stakes 100-piece decision you'll make. You're committing cash to test a factory you've never produced with, on a fiber (cashmere) where quality variance is invisible to the untrained eye, for products you'll need to scale to 500-5,000 pieces if the samples work. Most first-time buyers get the sample policy wrong — they pay too much, wait too long, or accept sample terms that don't protect them when bulk order time comes.

This guide walks through everything a B2B buyer should know about cashmere sample policies, based on our factory's experience shipping 800+ sample batches per year to 500+ brands across EU, US, Japan, Korea, and Australia. We cover the typical cost ranges (free vs paid, custom vs stock), realistic lead times by sample type, what should be in writing before you pay a sample fee, and how to structure the bulk-conversion discount that turns your sample cost into a recoverable deposit.

## Why Cashmere Samples Are Different from Cotton or Polyester Samples

Cashmere samples are more expensive for three structural reasons:

**Reason 1: Yarn minimums.** Most cashmere factories don't dye or spin yarn in single-meter quantities. To make a single garment sample, we need to dye at least 0.5-1 kg of yarn (sufficient for 3-5 pullovers depending on gauge), even if you only want one sample. The setup cost is amortized over fewer pieces than for cotton, where sample runs of 1-2 meters are economical.

**Reason 2: Knitting setup time.** A 12-gauge flat knitting machine takes 2-4 hours to set up for a new design (yarn threading, tension calibration, pattern programming). For one sample, that's 2-4 hours of machine time to produce 30 minutes' worth of actual knitting. The setup cost gets loaded into per-sample pricing.

**Reason 3: Hand-finishing labor.** Cashmere garments require hand-linking at seams (the panels are knitted separately, then sewn together by hand), hand-washing, and hand-pressing before they're "finished." Each sample takes 1-2 hours of skilled hand labor. A single cashmere sweater sample thus costs USD 30-80 in labor alone, even before yarn cost.

These structural costs mean that "free samples" almost never means free for cashmere — even the most generous factory recovers sample cost somewhere, whether through per-sample fees, sample-MOQ thresholds, or bulk-conversion discounts.

## The 4 Types of Cashmere Samples

**Type 1: Stock sample (cheapest, fastest)**
- A factory's existing color, yarn count, and standard fit
- Lead time: 3-5 days
- Cost: USD 50-150 per sample (covers yarn + labor amortized)
- Refund: 50-100% refundable against bulk order of USD 5,000+
- Best for: initial feasibility check (does this factory make the product category I need?)

**Type 2: Stock color, custom fit (mid-range)**
- Factory's standard yarn but with your measurements or fit preferences
- Lead time: 7-12 days (plus 3-5 days for pattern making)
- Cost: USD 100-200 per sample
- Refund: 50-100% refundable against bulk order of USD 5,000+
- Best for: fit verification before custom color

**Type 3: Custom color, stock fit (premium)**
- Your Pantone color but factory's standard fit pattern
- Lead time: 12-18 days (includes lab dip 5-7 days)
- Cost: USD 120-250 per sample (includes custom dye lot setup)
- Refund: 50-100% refundable against bulk order of USD 8,000+
- Best for: brand color verification before going custom across the line

**Type 4: Fully custom (color + fit + design)**
- Custom Pantone, custom pattern, custom design details (trims, labels, packaging)
- Lead time: 18-30 days
- Cost: USD 150-300 per sample (sometimes higher for complex designs)
- Refund: 25-50% refundable against bulk order of USD 10,000+
- Best for: full sample run before first production order

**Type 4 also known as:** "Salesman sample" or "pre-production sample" in industry jargon.

## What Every Cashmere Sample Order Should Include in Writing

Whether you order a single stock sample or a fully custom sample, these 4 clauses should be in the sample agreement before you pay. Most factories provide them automatically; if yours doesn't, ask before paying the sample fee.

**Clause 1: Exact sample specification**
- Yarn count (e.g. 2/26 Nm, 2/48 Nm, 2/60 Nm)
- Micron (e.g. 14.5μm, 15.5μm, 16.5μm)
- Color (Pantone TPX or factory color code)
- Gauge (e.g. 12-gauge, 14-gauge, 16-gauge)
- Size set requested (e.g. M only, or XS-S-M-L-XL full set)
- Quantity (e.g. 1 piece per color, 3 pieces per color, etc.)
- Hand-finishing requirements (hand-linked vs machine-linked seams)

**Clause 2: Lead time and shipping terms**
- Sample ready date (not "approximately" — a specific date)
- Shipping method (DHL, FedEx, UPS by air; or sea if you can wait 25-30 days)
- Shipping cost responsibility (factory-paid vs buyer-paid vs split)
- Door-to-door delivery date or port-of-arrival date

**Clause 3: Refund terms against bulk order**
- Sample fee refund percentage (50%, 80%, 100%?)
- Minimum bulk order value for refund eligibility (USD 5,000? 10,000?)
- Time limit for sample-fee conversion (typically 60-90 days from sample approval)
- Whether refund applies as credit memo or actual refund
- Whether refund applies to the full sample fee or only the "labor + yarn" portion (custom dye setup fees are often non-refundable)

**Clause 4: Defect and re-sample terms**
- What happens if the sample arrives defective (visible defect, wrong color, wrong size)
- Re-sample production cost: free, charged, or split?
- Re-sample lead time: same as original or faster?
- Maximum number of re-samples (some factories limit re-samples to 2 rounds)

If any of these are missing from the sample agreement, ask before paying. The factory that refuses to put these in writing is also the factory that will argue every point when bulk order time comes.

## Lead Time Benchmarks by Sample Type

These are the lead times **after** the factory receives your spec confirmation and sample fee. Add 3-5 days for international air shipping from Asia.

| Sample Type | Production Lead Time | Shipping to EU/US/JP |
|------|----------|----------------|
| Stock sample | 3-5 days | 3-5 days |
| Custom fit only | 7-12 days | 3-5 days |
| Custom color only | 12-18 days | 3-5 days |
| Fully custom (color + fit + design) | 18-30 days | 3-5 days |

**Why "stock sample" can be faster than custom color:** Stock samples use yarn that's already dyed and on cones; custom color requires a lab dip (5-7 days) plus a small dye lot. The lab dip alone is half the lead time.

**B2B tip:** If you're evaluating 3-5 factories, order stock samples from all of them in parallel (3-5 day lead time each), then order custom samples only from the 1-2 you shortlist. This compresses total sampling time by 1-2 weeks.

## Refund Strategy: How to Negotiate 100% Refundable Samples

Most factories will give a 100% sample fee refund on stock samples for first-time buyers, because the risk is low (no custom dye, no pattern development). For custom samples, the refund percentage drops because the factory invests real engineering time. Here's how to maximize your refund:

**Strategy 1: Commit to a bulk order value up front.**
If you tell the factory "we plan to place a USD 20,000-50,000 bulk order within 90 days if sample works," the factory's risk calculation changes. They'll quote 100% refundable because they expect to recover the sample cost in bulk.

**Strategy 2: Pay for samples in advance but negotiate conversion credit.**
"100% refundable against bulk order of USD 10,000 within 90 days" is the typical best-case clause. Even if you only place a USD 6,000 bulk order, ask for partial conversion (60-80%) as a goodwill gesture.

**Strategy 3: Order multiple sample types at once.**
If you order 3 colors × 1 piece each (3 pieces total) instead of 1 piece at a time, the factory amortizes setup cost over 3 pieces and can offer lower per-sample pricing or higher refund percentage.

**Strategy 4: Time the sample order before a seasonal production window.**
Factories have slack periods (typically Feb-April and Aug-Sept, between major retail seasons). Samples ordered during slack periods may get faster turnaround and better refund terms, because the factory wants to keep its line busy.

**Strategy 5: Avoid "free sample" promises from new or unknown factories.**
If a factory you've never worked with offers a "free sample" with no clear conversion terms, ask why. Often this signals either (a) the factory is desperate for the order and may have quality issues, or (b) the sample will be low quality and the "free" is offset by future hidden costs.

## The Sample-to-Bulk Conversion Pipeline: What Should Happen After Sample Approval

Once you approve the sample, three things should happen in sequence:

**Step 1: Quotation for bulk order**
- Factory should provide per-piece pricing at 100 / 300 / 500 / 1,000 / 2,000 piece tiers
- Pricing should be inclusive of yarn, knitting, hand-linking, finishing, QC, packaging
- Pricing should specify the FOB / CIF / DDP Incoterm and which costs are included

**Step 2: Bulk production plan and lead time**
- Factory should specify production lead time by tier (e.g. "100-piece order: 25-30 days production; 1,000-piece order: 30-40 days production")
- Plus shipping lead time (sea: 25-35 days to EU/US; air: 5-7 days)
- Total door-to-door for a 1,000-piece order: 55-75 days

**Step 3: Sampling credit conversion**
- You place bulk order
- Factory issues credit memo for the sample fee (full or partial per agreement)
- Credit is applied to your bulk invoice

If any of these three steps don't happen automatically after sample approval, follow up within 48 hours. A factory that doesn't proactively communicate sample-to-bulk logistics is one that will have communication problems during bulk production too.

## Red Flags in Cashmere Sample Policies

**Red flag 1: "Sample fees are non-refundable."**
This is acceptable for fully custom designs with expensive tooling, but unusual for stock or stock-color-custom-fit samples. If a factory refuses any sample-fee conversion on a standard product, question whether they're competitive enough to win your bulk order.

**Red flag 2: "Minimum sample order is 5 pieces per color."**
Some factories force a 5-piece minimum per color for "quality reasons." The real reason is usually that they want to lock you into a larger commitment before you've validated the product. A 1-piece sample per color is standard.

**Red flag 3: "Samples take 30-45 days."**
Standard stock samples take 3-5 days; fully custom samples take 18-30 days. If a factory quotes 30-45 days for a stock sample, they're either slow or pricing your sample at a higher tier (probably Type 3 or 4). Confirm what's included.

**Red flag 4: "Sample shipping is on buyer account, USD 50-150 minimum."**
Some factories charge excessive shipping fees for small samples. The real DHL/FedEx cost for a 1-2 kg sample from China to EU/US is USD 25-50. USD 50-150 minimum is a hidden markup. Ask for the shipping invoice.

**Red flag 5: "No re-samples if you're not satisfied."**
This signals the factory doesn't expect to invest in problem resolution. Any reputable factory offers at least 1 round of re-sampling for defects or wrong-spec issues.
