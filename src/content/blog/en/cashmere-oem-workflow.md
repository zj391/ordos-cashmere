# Cashmere OEM/ODM Workflow: From Sketch to Shipment in 8-12 Weeks (2026)

For B2B buyers looking to launch a private-label cashmere brand or extend an existing product line, the cashmere OEM/ODM (Original Equipment Manufacturer / Original Design Manufacturer) process is a 8-12 week journey that touches every part of the supply chain, from initial design concept to final shipment at your warehouse. Understanding the timeline, the deliverables at each stage, and the costs involved is critical for setting realistic launch dates and managing inventory.

This guide walks through the complete cashmere OEM/ODM workflow based on the 23 years of our Ordos factory's experience producing 80-100 OEM collections per year for 500+ B2B brands across EU/US/Japan.

## Phase 1: Design and Tech Pack (Week 1)

The OEM/ODM process begins with the buyer's design concept, expressed as a tech pack. A tech pack is a document that communicates every detail of the garment: silhouette, measurements, fabric specifications, color, hardware, label placement, and packaging requirements. We provide a standard tech pack template that covers the essentials, and our design team can also create a tech pack from scratch if you have sketches or reference images but no formal specifications.

**Deliverables in Week 1:**
- Tech pack: silhouette sketch + measurement spec sheet (5-10 measurements per garment, depending on style)
- Fabric specification: cashmere grade (Grade A/B/C), micron count, yarn count, knit gauge, weight per square meter
- Color reference: Pantone code, lab dip, or physical swatch
- Hardware and label specs: buttons, zippers, woven labels, leather patches, custom hangtags, custom packaging
- Quantity plan: total order quantity, size breakdown (XS/S/M/L/XL), color count, lead time target

For first-time OEM clients, the tech pack creation takes 2-5 business days. Established brands with prior collections can submit a tech pack in 1-2 days. Our design team reviews every tech pack for manufacturability: we'll flag if a sleeve length is too long for the knit gauge, if a color isn't available in the requested yarn count, or if a custom hardware requires a 4-week tooling lead time.

**Cost in Phase 1**: Free for tech pack review. If our design team creates the tech pack from your sketches or images, the cost is USD 50-150 per design, refundable against bulk order.

## Phase 2: Pattern Development (Week 1-2)

Once the tech pack is approved, our pattern makers convert the 2D design into 3D digital patterns using CAD software (Gerber, Lectra, or Assyst). The pattern includes every panel of the garment, the stitch types at each seam, the seam allowances, and the nesting layout for fabric utilization. For a typical women's pullover, the pattern has 8-12 panels; for a more complex garment like a wrap coat, it can be 20+ panels.

**Deliverables in Week 2:**
- Digital pattern file (.dxf or Gerber format)
- Marker (nesting layout) for fabric utilization
- Sample cut plan showing how many fabric meters per garment size
- Yarn order: 2/26 Nm, 2/48 Nm, or custom count

Pattern development is the most iterative part of the process. Most cashmere garments require 2-3 rounds of sample refinement to get the fit right: the first sample is a "fit sample" using substitute yarn to check silhouette and proportions, the second is a "salesman sample" using actual cashmere yarn, and the third is a "pre-production sample" with final fabric, trims, and labels.

**Cost in Phase 2**: USD 80-200 per design for digital pattern development. If a first-time client has no existing pattern, we charge a one-time pattern fee of USD 50-100 per size per style, refundable against bulk order of 200+ pieces.

## Phase 3: Counter-Sample Production (Week 2-3)

The counter-sample is the first physical garment made from the digital pattern, used to verify the pattern, fit, and construction. We typically make 1-3 counter-samples per design, depending on how many rounds of refinement the client wants. The first counter-sample uses substitute yarn (often merino wool or lower-grade cashmere) to keep cost down; the second uses actual production yarn.

**Deliverables in Week 3:**
- 1-3 counter-samples shipped to the client via DHL/FedEx/UPS (5-7 days to EU/US)
- Detailed fit comments: shoulders, sleeves, body length, neckline, hem
- Construction review: seam quality, stitch type, finishing
- Updated measurements based on actual garment (vs. tech pack target)

Counter-sample lead time is 7-10 days for the first sample, 5-7 days for each subsequent revision. Most brands need 2 rounds of counter-samples to finalize fit, pushing the total to 14-17 days.

**Cost in Phase 3**: USD 80-150 per counter-sample (depending on yarn count and garment complexity). For pure cashmere samples in 2/48 Nm 14.5μm, expect USD 100-150 per sample. Sampling cost is typically refundable against bulk order.

## Phase 4: Salesman Sample (Week 3-4)

Once fit is approved on the counter-sample, we produce a salesman sample using the actual production yarn, trims, and labels. The salesman sample is used for marketing photos, buyer presentations, trade show displays, and pre-orders. It's typically higher quality than the counter-sample because the factory is now running the actual production setup.

**Deliverables in Week 4:**
- 1-3 salesman samples using final cashmere yarn
- Photos: high-resolution images on mannequin or live model (optional, USD 50-200 per style)
- Pre-production sample shipped to client for final approval before bulk

Salesman sample lead time is 7-10 days. The factory is now ready to schedule bulk production, but waits for the client's "PP (pre-production) sample approved" sign-off before starting.

**Cost in Phase 4**: USD 100-200 per salesman sample. Higher than counter-sample because actual production yarn + trims + labels are used.

## Phase 5: Bulk Production (Week 4-8)

Once the salesman sample is approved, bulk production begins. The 4-week window covers:
- Week 4: yarn procurement and dyeing
- Week 5-6: knitting (or weaving for woven products)
- Week 6-7: linking (sewing panels together) and finishing
- Week 7-8: QC and packing

For larger orders (1,000+ pieces per style), production may extend to 5-6 weeks due to machine capacity constraints. For smaller orders (100-300 pieces), production is often 2-3 weeks.

**Deliverables during Bulk Production:**
- Pre-shipment sample: 3-5 random samples pulled from the production line and shipped to the client for final QC approval before shipping the full order
- Production photos: factory-floor photos of knitting machines, linking lines, and finished goods (optional, USD 50-150 per style)
- Inspection report: AQL 2.5 sample inspection, listing any defects found and the resolution
- Packing list: exact carton count, weight, dimensions

**Capacity**: A single knitting machine produces 80-120 cashmere sweaters per day. For a 500-piece order across 5 styles (100 pieces each), 4-5 knitting machines run in parallel for 1 day each, with linking and finishing taking another week.

**Cost in Phase 5**: This is where the bulk order pricing kicks in. The per-piece cost is typically:
- USD 38-50 for women's 2/48 Nm 14.5μm pullover in stock colors, 100+ pieces
- USD 50-75 for men's 2/26 Nm 14.5μm pullover with custom Pantone color, 100+ pieces
- USD 75-120 for 14.5μm 12-gauge intarsia design, 200+ pieces
- USD 120-180 for 14.5μm 16-gauge fully-fashioned fine-gauge pullover, 200+ pieces

The first 50 pieces are typically 1.5-2x the bulk rate due to setup cost amortization. Order 100 pieces and the per-piece cost drops to the bulk rate shown above.

## Phase 6: QC and Pre-Shipment Inspection (Week 8-9)

Before shipping, every order goes through QC. Our standard process:
- AQL 2.5 sampling: 32 pieces inspected for orders 501-1,200, 50 pieces for 1,201-3,200
- Inspection covers: measurements vs. tech pack, stitch quality, color consistency, label placement, packaging integrity
- Defects classified: critical (must reject), major (must repair), minor (acceptable)
- Photo documentation of any defects with the resolution (repair / replace / accept with discount)

**Third-party inspection** is available for an additional cost. We coordinate with SGS, Intertek, Bureau Veritas, and ITS. Pre-shipment inspection (PSI) costs USD 250-400 per man-day, typically 1-2 days for cashmere garment orders. The inspector flies to our Ordos facility, performs the AQL inspection, and sends a report within 48 hours.

**Cost in Phase 6**: In-house QC is included in the bulk price. Third-party PSI is USD 250-400 per man-day, plus inspector's travel and accommodation (typically USD 800-1,500 per inspection).

## Phase 7: Shipping and Customs (Week 9-12)

Once the QC is passed and the client approves the pre-shipment sample, we pack the order in export cartons (5-ply corrugated, ISPM-15 compliant, fumigation-free). Each carton typically holds 20-30 cashmere sweaters in polybags, 10-20 in master polybags. The order is then shipped via the agreed Incoterm (FOB, CIF, or DDP).

**Lead times:**
- FOB Tianjin: 3-5 days for factory packing + 1-2 days for port handling
- Sea freight to EU/US: 25-35 days
- Air freight to EU/US: 5-7 days
- DDP door-to-door: 25-35 days sea freight + 3-7 days last-mile delivery

**Cost in Phase 7**: Sea freight USD 80-200 per cubic meter depending on destination. Air freight USD 8-15 per kg. Customs duties and VAT are paid by the buyer under FOB/CIF, by the factory under DDP.

## Phase 8: Post-Delivery Support (Week 12+)

After delivery, the OEM/ODM process is largely complete, but ongoing support includes:
- Quality issue resolution: defects reported within 90 days are replaced free of charge
- Reorder: minimum 100 pieces, 4-6 week lead time, with pattern + yarn already approved
- New collection development: 2-3 month timeline for new designs based on the same patterns
- Marketing support: high-resolution product photos, video footage, factory tour for content

## Total OEM/ODM Timeline: 8-12 Weeks

For a first-time OEM collection with 5-7 designs and 100-300 pieces per design, the realistic timeline is:
- Week 1: Tech pack and design
- Week 2-3: Pattern development + counter-samples
- Week 3-4: Salesman samples
- Week 4-8: Bulk production
- Week 8-9: QC and pre-shipment inspection
- Week 9-12: Shipping and customs clearance

**Total: 8-12 weeks from sketch to warehouse**

For repeat OEM orders (the same brand, same patterns, same yarn), the timeline compresses to 5-7 weeks because the pattern is already approved, the yarn is in stock, and the production setup is faster. Established brands often run 4-5 OEM collections per year with this compressed timeline.

## Common OEM Pitfalls and How to Avoid Them

**Pitfall 1: Tech pack too vague.** "A cashmere sweater" is not enough. Specify the gauge (12, 14, 16?), the weight per square meter (180, 220, 280?), the micron count (14.5, 15.5?), the yarn count (2/26, 2/48, 2/60?), the color (Pantone 19-3920 Navy, not just "navy"), and the fit (slim, regular, relaxed?). The more detail, the fewer revisions needed.

**Pitfall 2: Sampling rounds > 3.** If you're on the 4th sample revision, the pattern or tech pack has a fundamental issue. Stop, review the fit comments holistically, and identify the most critical changes. Three sample rounds is industry standard; four is a sign of unclear direction.

**Pitfall 3: No pre-production sample approval gate.** Some clients want to skip the pre-production sample and go straight to bulk. This is risky: if the bulk doesn't match the salesman sample, you eat the cost. Always require a pre-production sample sign-off before bulk starts.

**Pitfall 4: Ignoring capacity at peak season.** September-January is peak season for cashmere production (retailers stocking for winter). Capacity is fully booked, lead times extend, and per-piece prices are 5-10% higher. Plan OEM collections 4-6 months ahead of peak.

**Pitfall 5: Skipping third-party inspection for first orders.** For first orders over USD 50,000, third-party PSI is worth the 0.5-1% additional cost. The inspector catches issues that the factory's in-house QC might miss (or hide). Once you've built a relationship with the factory, you can skip PSI for repeat orders under USD 100,000.
