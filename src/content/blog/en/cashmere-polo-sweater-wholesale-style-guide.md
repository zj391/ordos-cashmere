# Cashmere Polo Sweater Wholesale: B2B Style Guide (Polo vs Crew vs Mock Neck Comparison)

Cashmere polo-collar sweaters have become one of the most-requested knit constructions at our Ordos facility over the past two FW seasons. The reason is structural: the polo collar (2–3 button placket, fold-over or ribbed knit collar) occupies a niche between casual and refined that the standard crew neck and the V-neck cardigan don't quite fill. For private-label buyers building collections at the $160–320 retail tier, the polo construction is increasingly a core style — not a one-off curiosity.

This guide compares polo, crew, mock neck, and V-neck constructions so B2B buyers can choose the right collar for their brand positioning.

## When to Choose Cashmere Polo

The polo construction works best for these B2B programs:

- **Smart-casual retail programs** targeting 30–55 year-old professional buyers
- **Resort and country-club collections** where a fold collar signals refinement
- **Corporate-casual uniform programs** where a polo replaces a formal shirt collar
- **Bridal and occasionwear programs** where the polo construction works under a jacket

Avoid the polo for:
- **Athleisure or loungewear** programs (use crew or hooded)
- **Youth or streetwear** programs (use mock neck or hooded)
- **Programs below $140 retail** (the cost premium for polo construction won't absorb)

## Construction Details

**Placket**: 2-button (most common, modern), 3-button (classic), or zip-front (rare, premium athleisure). 2-button polo is the most-requested at our facility.

**Collar**: fold-over knit collar (most common) or ribbed knit band collar (sportier). Fold-over is the standard for cashmere; ribbed band is reserved for merino or cashmere-blend sport polos.

**Placket finish**: reinforced placket with 1.5cm turnover, bar-tacked at top and bottom. This is a precision detail — buyers should request placket samples before bulk production to confirm the finish matches brand quality standards.

**Buttons**: standard 12–14 Ligne horn buttons (most common), mother-of-pearl (premium), corozo (eco), or plastic (economy). Button material is a design choice; we recommend matching the program tier (horn for cashmere polo at $200+ retail).

## Knit Gauge and Yarn

**Gauge 7gg – 9gg (chunky)**: 320–420g polo weight, visible knit texture, premium casual aesthetic. Best for gift-set polo programs.

**Gauge 10gg – 12gg (mid)**: 240–320g polo weight, smooth surface, balanced drape. Standard for commercial cashmere polo production.

**Gauge 14gg (fine)**: 180–240g polo weight, refined drape, dressier aesthetic. Common for premium athleisure-tier polos.

**Yarn**: 2/26 Nm woolen-spun (most common) or 2/48 Nm worsted-spun (premium tier). Worsted-spun adds 30–40% to yarn cost.

## Common Mistakes B2B Buyers Make

**Mistake 1: Specifying a 7gg polo for a refined retail program.** The chunky knit texture reads as casual and contradicts the polo's smart-casual positioning. Use 10gg – 12gg for refined retail programs; reserve 7gg for casual or gift-set programs.

**Mistake 2: Skipping the placket finish sample.** The 1.5cm turnover and bar-tack detail is where the polo construction visibly differs from a budget version. Always request a placket close-up sample.

**Mistake 3: Specifying plastic buttons on a $200+ retail polo.** The button material is the most visible detail on a polo collar. Horn or mother-of-pearl matches the cashmere fiber; plastic breaks the brand promise.

**Mistake 4: Underestimating the placket yarn consumption.** The placket, button placket band, and collar band together add 25–35g of yarn per polo versus a crew neck. Budget this in for both cost and lead-time calculations.
