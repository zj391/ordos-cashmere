## What changed in July 2026

On 9 February 2026, the European Commission announced delegated and implementing measures under the Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation (ESPR) concerning unsold consumer products. The Commission states that the ban on destroying unsold apparel, clothing accessories and footwear applies to large companies from **19 July 2026**; medium-sized companies are expected to follow in 2030. [1](https://environment.ec.europa.eu/news/new-eu-rules-stop-destruction-unsold-clothes-and-shoes-2026-02-09_en)

The same Commission update says that large companies are already subject to disclosure requirements relating to discarded unsold consumer products, while the standardised disclosure format applies from February 2027. The legal details, derogations and company scope should be checked against the applicable regulations rather than inferred from a product category alone. [1](https://environment.ec.europa.eu/news/new-eu-rules-stop-destruction-unsold-clothes-and-shoes-2026-02-09_en)

## Why this matters to a cashmere sourcing brief

This change is not a claim about any particular brand, importer or supplier. It is a reason for parties that place apparel or accessories on the EU market to ask more precise questions about item identity, inventory status and end-of-life decisions. A cashmere program may involve finished knitwear, scarves, hats, gloves or other accessories; the applicable legal duties depend on the responsible economic operator and the facts of that program.

The Commission says the measures clarify limited, justified circumstances in which destruction may be permitted, such as safety reasons or product damage, and that national authorities will oversee compliance. [1](https://environment.ec.europa.eu/news/new-eu-rules-stop-destruction-unsold-clothes-and-shoes-2026-02-09_en) A sourcing partner should therefore not use a generic “circular” statement in place of a documented decision process.

## Records worth discussing before production

| Project record | Question to agree | Avoid assuming |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Style identification | Can the approved style, color and size range be identified consistently? | That a style code alone proves legal compliance. |
| Quantity records | Which party maintains order, shipment, return and unsold-goods records? | That the upstream maker controls the importer’s inventory. |
| Defect and safety process | How are damaged, non-conforming or safety-related goods recorded and reviewed? | That every damaged item can be handled in the same way. |
| Alternative pathways | Which resale, repair, donation, reuse or recycling options are commercially and legally available? | That a pathway is available in every destination. |
| Responsibility map | Who is responsible for the applicable EU disclosure or prohibition requirements? | That responsibility follows the purchase order automatically. |

The value of these records is operational clarity. They can help a buyer separate product specification, inventory ownership, returns handling and environmental communication before a program launches. They do not replace legal advice, national guidance or a contract allocation of responsibility.

## A useful August 2026 review question

For a new EU-facing cashmere program, ask the brand or importer: *Which entity places the finished goods on the EU market, and what information will that entity need from its suppliers to support its unsold-goods process?* The answer can be translated into a project-specific documentation list rather than a broad claim about disposal, returns or circularity.
