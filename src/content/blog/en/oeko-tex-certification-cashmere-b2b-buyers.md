# OEKO-TEX 100 for Cashmere: What B2B Buyers Actually Need vs Nice-to-Have

## Introduction

When sourcing cashmere for your brand or supply chain, certifications are more than badges—they're risk management tools. Among the most requested is OEKO-TEX Standard 100, a global testing and certification system for textile raw materials, intermediate, and end products. But not all OEKO-TEX requirements carry equal weight for B2B buyers.

In this guide, we break down what you **actually need** to verify for chemical safety, compliance, and market access, versus what's **nice-to-have** but may not justify cost or lead time premiums. We draw on over two decades of experience as a cashmere source factory based in Ordos, Inner Mongolia, where the world's finest cashmere originates.

## Understanding OEKO-TEX 100 for Cashmere

OEKO-TEX Standard 100 tests for harmful substances across four product classes:

- **Class I:** Products for babies (under 36 months)
- **Class II:** Products with direct skin contact
- **Class III:** Products without direct skin contact
- **Class IV:** Decoration materials

For cashmere, most B2B buyers target **Class I** or **Class II**, depending on end use. Class I has the strictest limits on chemicals like formaldehyde, heavy metals, pesticides, and pH levels.

### What OEKO-TEX Actually Tests in Cashmere

| Substance Group | Example | Why It Matters for Cashmere |
|-----------------|---------|-----------------------------|
| Pesticides | Lindane, DDT | Cashmere goats may be treated with external pesticides; residues can persist in fiber |
| Heavy metals | Lead, cadmium, mercury | From dyeing and finishing agents |
| Formaldehyde | Resin finishes | Used in anti-shrink or anti-pilling treatments |
| Chlorinated phenols | PCP, TeCP | Preservatives in fiber storage |
| Allergen dyes | Disperse dyes | Common in colored cashmere yarns |
| pH value | 4.0–7.5 (Class I) | Skin irritation risk |

## What B2B Buyers Actually Need: The Must-Haves

### 1. Class I Certification for Baby & Children's Cashmere

If your product touches infant or toddler skin (e.g., cashmere onesies, blankets), **Class I is non-negotiable**. Many EU and Japanese retailers require it by law or internal policy. Without it, you risk customs rejection or delisting.

**DONGXIAO tip:** Our Class I certified cashmere yarns and garments undergo batch testing at an independent OEKO-TEX accredited lab in Shanghai. We provide test reports per production lot—not just a blanket certificate.

### 2. Verification of Testing Frequency

A certificate is only as good as the testing behind it. Ask your supplier:

- How often are raw fibers tested? (Annually? Per season?)
- Are finished garments tested separately from yarn?
- Do you test for all 100+ parameters or a subset?

**Reality check:** Many factories test only raw material once a year. For B2B buyers, we recommend requesting **production batch testing** for Class I or II items, especially if you source from multiple origins.

### 3. Chemical Management in the Supply Chain

OEKO-TEX 100 requires that all chemical inputs (dyes, auxiliaries, finishes) are from approved suppliers. This is critical because cashmere dyeing often uses metal-complex dyes that can leave heavy metal residues.

**What to look for:** A supplier that maintains a **MRSL (Manufacturing Restricted Substances List)** and can trace chemicals back to the manufacturer. At DONGXIAO, we use only OEKO-TEX certified dyehouses in Inner Mongolia, and we audit them annually.

### 4. Documentation for Customs & Retailers

For EU and US markets, customs may request OEKO-TEX certificates for cashmere imports. Retailers like Zara, H&M, and luxury brands often mandate it in their supplier codes of conduct.

**Must-have documents:**
- Valid OEKO-TEX certificate (issued within last 12 months)
- Test report per product class
- Declaration of conformity from the manufacturer

## What's Nice-to-Have: The Optional Extras

### 1. Class I for Adult Cashmere

If your adult cashmere sweaters have direct skin contact, Class II is sufficient. Class I adds cost (stricter limits, more testing) but rarely adds market value. Only pursue Class I for adult items if your brand positions as "baby-safe" or "hypoallergenic."

### 2. Full Supply Chain Certification

OEKO-TEX offers a **STeP** certification for sustainable textile production facilities, and **Made in Green** for traceability. These are valuable for marketing but not required for basic compliance. Most B2B buyers start with Standard 100 and add STeP later.

**Cost consideration:** Full STeP certification can add $5,000–$15,000 per factory and require 6–12 months. For small to mid-sized buyers, it's often a nice-to-have.

### 3. GOTS + OEKO-TEX Dual Certification

Organic cashmere (GOTS) is a niche market. Combining GOTS and OEKO-TEX can differentiate your product, but it's rare in cashmere due to limited organic fiber supply. Less than 2% of global cashmere is GOTS-certified. If you need both, expect higher MOQs and longer lead times (60–90 days vs 30–45).

## How to Verify a Supplier's OEKO-TEX Claims

1. **Check the certificate number** on the OEKO-TEX database (www.oeko-tex.com).
2. **Look for the test lab name**—must be an accredited institute (e.g., Hohenstein, TESTEX).
3. **Request the test report** for your specific product class, not just a generic certificate.
4. **Ask about scope:** Is the certificate for the factory, the product, or the entire company?

**Real-world example:** A buyer once presented a certificate that covered only "cashmere yarn" but was using it for finished garments. The certificate was invalid for their end product. Always match the certificate scope to your product.

## Regional Considerations

- **EU:** OEKO-TEX 100 is de facto standard for cashmere imports. Some retailers also require REACH compliance.
- **Japan:** Japanese buyers often request OEKO-TEX 100 plus formaldehyde testing per JIS L 1041.
- **USA:** Not mandatory but preferred by major retailers. CPSIA compliance may also be needed for children's items.
- **China (domestic):** GB 18401 is the national standard; OEKO-TEX is seen as premium.

## Conclusion: Prioritize What Matters

For B2B cashmere buyers, **Class II certification is the baseline** for adult garments, and **Class I is mandatory for children's products**. Verify testing frequency, chemical management, and certificate validity. Nice-to-haves like STeP or GOTS can be added later for brand differentiation.
