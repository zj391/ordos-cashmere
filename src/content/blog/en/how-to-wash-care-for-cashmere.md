# How to Wash, Dry, and Store Cashmere: A Factory's Honest Care Guide

Cashmere is a natural fiber. Treat it well and it lasts 10+ years. Treat it poorly and it pills, shrinks, or loses shape in 6 months. After 23 years of watching how customers care for their cashmere, here's the honest guide.

## The 3 ways to wash cashmere (ranked by safety)

**1. Hand wash (safest)** — Always works. Use cool water (30°C max) and a pH-neutral wool wash (we like Eucalan or Soak, no rinsing required). Submerge for 15-20 minutes. Gently press (don't wring) the water out. Roll in a clean towel to remove excess moisture. Lay flat to dry on a fresh towel, reshaping gently.

**2. Machine wash (acceptable, with conditions)** — Most modern washing machines have a "wool" or "delicates" cycle that mimics hand washing. Use:
- Cool water (cold setting, 30°C max)
- Wool-safe detergent (NOT regular detergent, NOT fabric softener)
- Mesh laundry bag (essential — prevents snagging and agitation damage)
- Lowest spin speed (or no spin)
- Wash similar colors together (camel + ivory are fine; white + dark = trouble)

**NEVER** use hot water, regular detergent, or high spin. Hot water + agitation = felted cashmere (irreversible shrinkage).

**3. Dry cleaning (works but with caveats)** — Most dry cleaners use perchloroethylene (perc) which doesn't damage cashmere but isn't great for the environment. Some "green" cleaners use CO2 or water-based methods. If you dry clean, request "gentle cycle" and "low heat". Avoid dry cleaning more than 2-3 times per year — the solvents strip natural oils from the fiber over time.

Our honest recommendation: **hand wash if you have 20 minutes, machine wash on wool cycle if you're in a hurry, dry clean only for stubborn stains.**

## Drying cashmere (this is where most people ruin their sweaters)

**THE BIGGEST MISTAKE:** Hanging a wet cashmere sweater to dry. The water weight stretches the fiber, leaving the shoulders deformed and the body elongated — permanently.

**Correct method:**
1. After washing, gently press water out (don't wring)
2. Roll in a clean dry towel, press to absorb (repeat with second dry towel if very wet)
3. Lay flat on a fresh dry towel, reshape to original dimensions
4. Keep away from direct sunlight and direct heat (radiators, hair dryers)
5. Flip after 8-12 hours; should be dry in 24-36 hours

Drying takes patience. **Never** put cashmere in a dryer (even on low heat). Even 10 minutes in a tumble dryer can cause irreversible shrinkage and felting.

## Pilling: what causes it, how to fix it

Pilling (those tiny fiber balls on the surface) is **not a quality defect** — it's a natural response to friction. New cashmere sweaters pill more in the first 3-4 wears as loose fibers come to the surface. After the initial pilling phase, good cashmere pills minimally for years.

**Where pilling happens most:**
- Underarm area (most friction from arm movement)
- Inner elbow (bending friction)
- Side torso (bag/strap friction)
- Cuffs (desk/friction contact)

**How to remove pills safely:**
- **Cashmere comb** (best tool) — fine-toothed comb specifically designed for knitwear. Lay the sweater flat, comb gently in one direction.
- **Fabric shaver** (also good) — electric de-piller like the Philips Fabric Shaver or Conair Fabric Defuzzer. Use on low setting, light pressure. Don't go over the same spot repeatedly.
- **Avoid:** Regular razors (cuts the fiber), tape (pulls fibers), scissors (cuts too much).

**How to prevent pilling long-term:**
- Wash inside out (reduces surface friction)
- Don't wear the same cashmere 2 days in a row (fiber needs 24 hours to recover)
- Store folded, not hung (hanging stretches shoulders)
- Use a cashmere wash (preserves natural oils)

A cashmere sweater that pills heavily in year 1 is a quality red flag. But pilling lightly in months 1-3, then stabilizing, is normal — even for Grade A.

## Stain removal (specific stain types)

**Oil-based stains** (makeup, salad dressing, butter): Sprinkle cornstarch or baby powder on the stain immediately. Let sit 30 minutes to absorb. Then hand wash with wool detergent. Repeat if needed. Do NOT use hot water first — sets the stain.

**Water-based stains** (coffee, wine, juice): Blot (don't rub) with cold water from the inside of the fabric. Apply a tiny amount of wool detergent, work in gently. Rinse. Repeat if needed.

**Deodorant marks:** Mix white vinegar and water 50/50. Dab on the mark, let sit 10 minutes, then wash normally.

**Moth holes:** Prevention is everything — see storage below. If you find a hole, take it to a tailor for repair. A skilled tailor can reweave small holes invisibly.

## Storage (off-season + daily)

**Daily storage:** Fold, don't hang. Hanging stretches shoulders permanently. Use a shelf or drawer with room for the sweater to "breathe" — don't cram it in.

**Off-season storage** (3+ months not worn):
1. Wash before storing (body oils and food residue attract moths)
2. Completely dry (moisture + closed storage = mildew)
3. Use **cedar blocks, lavender sachets, or moth balls** (naphthalene) — DO NOT use plastic garment bags for long-term storage; cashmere needs to breathe
4. Store in a **cotton storage bag** (not plastic) in a cool, dry place
5. Check every 2-3 months for moth activity

**Moth prevention is critical.** Clothes moths (Tineola bisselliella) lay eggs in natural fibers — including cashmere. A single moth can lay 100+ eggs, and the larvae eat cashmere fiber. If you see one moth, check ALL your cashmere immediately.

## When to retire a cashmere sweater

With proper care, a Grade A cashmere sweater should last 8-15 years. Signs it's time to retire:
- **Holes that can't be invisibly repaired** (large, in visible spots)
- **Permanent felting** (the fiber has matted together — usually from a hot wash incident)
- **Major pilling that doesn't respond to combing** (usually means the fiber is wearing through)

Most "cashmere ruined" stories involve one of three things: hot water wash, tumble dryer, or stored with body oils attracting moths. Avoid those three and your cashmere will outlive most of your wardrobe.
