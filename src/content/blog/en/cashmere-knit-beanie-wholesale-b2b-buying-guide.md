# Cashmere Knit Beanie vs Wool Beanie: B2B Buying Guide for Wholesale Buyers (FW 2026)

Every autumn, the same question arrives from private-label buyers and boutique owners across the EU, US, and Japan: *"Should we run a cashmere beanie program this year, or is wool close enough?"* The honest answer depends on what you are building — a premium gift, an everyday winter staple, or a corporate uniform — and the decision is rarely about fiber alone. It is about fiber **plus** gauge **plus** finishing **plus** minimum order quantity.

This guide walks through the engineering specs, pricing tiers, and sourcing realities that determine whether a cashmere knit beanie program makes sense for your brand. It is written from the perspective of an Ordos factory that produces both cashmere and merino wool beanies for 23+ years.

The single biggest practical difference is **hand-feel against the forehead**. Cashmere at 14.5μm has a fiber diameter roughly 30% thinner than fine merino. For buyers building premium gift sets or resort wear, this gap is the entire value proposition. For buyers building outdoor or uniform programs, the gap matters less and wool's durability may actually be preferable.

## When to Choose Cashmere

Cashmere knit beanies make sense for these B2B programs:

- **Premium gift sets** bundled with scarves, gloves, or shawls for luxury retail
- **Resort and travel wear** for high-end hotel chains, first-class airline partnerships
- **Boutique private-label collections** sold at $120–250 retail
- **Corporate executive gifting** for end-of-year client presents
- **Cashmere-only collections** where a wool piece would break the brand promise

If your retail price floor is below USD 60, cashmere is the wrong fiber. The economics break — your margin absorbs the cost differential before the customer experiences the hand-feel benefit.

## When to Choose Wool

Merino wool beanies work better for these B2B programs:

- **Outdoor and ski-resort uniform programs** where durability matters more than softness
- **School and team uniforms** at high volume (3,000+ pieces)
- **Eco-positioned brands** where the regenerative-wool story is part of the product
- **Mid-market retail** at USD 30–60 price points
- **Cold-weather workwear** for industrial and outdoor use

Wool is also the better choice when the program requires aggressive washing — merino tolerates machine washing on wool cycle far better than cashmere.

## Knit Construction: Gauge, Yarn Count, and Fit

Three numbers determine the final beanie: **gauge** (needles per inch), **yarn count** (Nm), and **crown height**.

**Gauge 7gg – 9gg (chunky knit)**: 38–55g beanie weight, visible rib texture, drapes softly. Best for cashmere and gift sets. Requires more yarn per piece, which raises fiber cost.

**Gauge 10gg – 12gg (mid-gauge)**: 32–45g beanie weight, balanced hand-feel, more shape-retention. The standard for both cashmere and wool commercial production.

**Gauge 14gg (fine-gauge)**: 24–35g beanie weight, smooth surface, dressier look. Most common for wool beanies sold at lower retail tiers. Rare for cashmere because the yarn becomes too thin to fully express the fiber benefit.

**Yarn count 2/26 Nm**: the industry standard for both fibers. Worsted-spun 2/48 Nm gives a smoother, more refined cashmere beanie for premium gift sets — but adds 30–40% to yarn cost.

**Crown height 18–22 cm**: standard. A 24cm "slouchy" beanie uses 12–18% more yarn and is a deliberate design choice for the youth market.

## Cuff, Fold, and Pom Options

Most cashmere beanies use a **folded cuff** (5–7cm turn-up at the bottom), which adds 8–12g of yarn but gives the wearer two wearing styles and signals premium quality. A **rib-knit cuff** (3–4cm, no fold) is more common on wool beanies for uniform programs.

Fur pom-poms are not used on cashmere beanies from our facility — the fiber and price point don't match the aesthetic. A **yarn pom** (matching cashmere or merino) is available from MOQ 200 pieces and adds $1.20–1.80 to the unit cost.

## Common Mistakes B2B Buyers Make

**Mistake 1: Specifying a 14gg cashmere beanie for a gift set.** The yarn is too thin to express cashmere's hand-feel benefit. Use 7gg – 10gg for gift-set programs.

**Mistake 2: Underestimating yarn consumption for cuffed beanies.** A cuffed cashmere beanie uses 18–25% more yarn than a rib-cuff version. Budget and MOQ calculations should account for this.

**Mistake 3: Ordering wool beanies for tropical markets.** A 60g wool beanie is uncomfortable above 25°C. For tropical retail or resort wear, specify 35g or lighter.

**Mistake 4: Skipping the OEKO-TEX certification request.** Premium retail increasingly requires OEKO-TEX Standard 100. Our facility holds certification and can include the certificate per shipment.
