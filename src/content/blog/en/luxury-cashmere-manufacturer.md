# Luxury Cashmere Manufacturer: What Separates a Premium Maker from a Commodity Factory

When buyers search "luxury cashmere manufacturer", they're not just shopping for cashmere — they're looking for a maker who can deliver the same quality as Loro Piana, Brunello Cucinelli, or Kiton. Those brands don't publicize their factories, but they all source from a small set of premium cashmere mills that meet specific standards.

This guide breaks down what actually separates a luxury-grade manufacturer from a commodity cashmere factory — based on 23 years of running one in Ordos.

## The 4 criteria that matter

### 1. Fiber grading: 14.0-15.5μm only

A luxury cashmere manufacturer starts with **Grade A fiber only** — 14.0-15.5μm diameter, 38mm+ length, 99.95% purity after dehairing.

| Grade | Micron range | Source | Price (USD/kg) | Use case |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | 14.0-15.5μm | Alashan, Arbas (Inner Mongolia) | $75-120 | Luxury garments |
| B | 15.5-16.5μm | Mixed Inner Mongolia | $50-75 | Mid-tier |
| C | 16.5-19.0μm | Lowland China, Iran, Afghanistan | $30-50 | Commodity |

If a factory doesn't disclose micron count or claims "100% cashmere" without grade, it's almost certainly using B or C fiber — possibly blended with wool.

### 2. Dehairing equipment: 4+ separator passes

The dehairing process removes coarse guard hair (>30μm) from soft down. **Premium mills run fiber through 4-6 separator passes** to hit 99.95% purity. Commodity dehairing often stops at 1-2 passes, leaving 5-15% guard hair that makes garments itchy.

Look for factories with **Worner or Laroche dehairing machines** (industry-standard European equipment). Domestic Chinese machines can produce acceptable yarn for lower-end garments but rarely hit luxury-grade purity.

### 3. Hand-linking vs machine-linking

Garment seaming is where most factories cut costs. A fully **hand-linked** cashmere sweater has each loop of the seam knitted to the corresponding loop of the garment piece — invisible joins, no bulk. A **machine-linked** sweater has a chain-stitched seam that's visible when you stretch the fabric.

Hand-linking adds 30-50% to labor cost. Luxury brands pay this; commodity brands skip it.

### 4. Dye chemistry: low-temperature metal-free dyes

Premium cashmere uses **low-temperature reactive dyes** (60-80°C dyeing bath) that don't damage fiber. Commodity mills use high-temperature acid dyes that strip the natural softness and shorten fiber life.

Certifications to look for:
- **OEKO-TEX Standard 100** Class I (baby-safe, strictest)
- **GOTS** (organic, also implies processing standards)
- **ZDHC** (Zero Discharge of Hazardous Chemicals)

## Red flags when sourcing from a "luxury" manufacturer

After 23 years, here are the questions that reveal a commodity factory in luxury clothing:

1. **"What's your micron spec?"** — If the answer is vague ("premium grade", "fine cashmere", "Grade AA"), it's probably 16μm+ blended with wool.
2. **"Can I see dehairing equipment photos?"** — Luxury mills are proud of their machinery; commodity factories won't show.
3. **"What's your hand-linking vs machine-linking ratio?"** — Real luxury is 90%+ hand-linked. Mass market is 100% machine-linked.
4. **"Show me your OEKO-TEX certificate with Class I."** — Most OEKO-TEX certificates are Class II (skin contact). Class I is baby-safe and stricter.
5. **"What's your reject rate on final inspection?"** — Luxury is 2-3% reject rate; mass market is 8-15%.

## What to expect from a true luxury cashmere manufacturer

A genuine luxury-grade manufacturer offers:
- **Fiber trace documentation**: which farm, which herd, which shearing season
- **Hand-feel samples**: 3-5 fabric swatches in different gauges (12gg, 14gg, 16gg) and weights
- **Garment samples** in 2-3 weeks with full measurement charts
- **Production capacity transparency**: most luxury mills max out at 2,000-5,000 sweaters/month
- **Direct contact with the factory owner or production manager**, not just sales reps
