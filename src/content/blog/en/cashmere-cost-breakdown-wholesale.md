# Why Cashmere Costs USD 8-50 per Piece: A Factory's True Cost Breakdown

"Why does cashmere cost so much?" is the most common question we get from new B2B buyers. The short answer: raw cashmere fiber alone costs USD 50-120 per kg, and a single sweater uses 300-500g of it. But the full picture involves more than just fiber.

Below is a **transparent cost breakdown** of a finished Grade A 14.5μm cashmere sweater, FOB Tianjin, for a 500-piece private label order. We've stripped out our margin so you can see what the actual cost structure looks like.

## The sweater we're costing

- **Product:** Crew-neck pullover, 14-gauge, 12 gg flat knit
- **Yarn:** 2/48 Nm worsted, 100% Grade A dehaired cashmere (14.5μm, 38mm)
- **Weight:** 380g per piece (medium-weight, men's M)
- **Order:** 500 pieces, 1 color (camel)
- **Incoterms:** FOB Tianjin

So a Grade A men's cashmere sweater costs about **USD 62 FOB Tianjin** for a 500-piece order. The breakdown shows why: **raw fiber alone is 51% of the cost**. This is why "cheap cashmere" is almost always a red flag — either the fiber is lower grade (15.5-19μm), or it's blended with wool or recycled fiber.

## What changes the price by 30-50%?

The biggest cost variables, in order of impact:

**1. Fiber grade (most impactful)**
- Grade A (14-15.5μm): USD 75-120/kg raw
- Grade B (15.5-16.5μm): USD 50-75/kg raw
- Grade C (16.5-19μm): USD 30-50/kg raw

A Grade B sweater of the same design: ~USD 42 FOB. A Grade C: ~USD 28 FOB.

**2. Order quantity (volume discount)**
- 100 pieces: ~USD 75 FOB (set-up costs amortized)
- 500 pieces: ~USD 62 FOB
- 2,000+ pieces: ~USD 52 FOB

The set-up costs (yarn lot minimum, machine programming, label production) are fixed regardless of order size.

**3. Gauge (yarn count)**
- 7gg chunky: heavier (500g+), more fiber per piece → ~USD 75 FOB
- 12gg standard: 380g → ~USD 62 FOB
- 16gg fine: 280g → ~USD 48 FOB

Finer gauge = less fiber = lower cost, but slower machine speed (more expensive knitting time per piece).

**4. Custom vs. stock design**
- Stock design (no programming): no extra cost
- Custom design (programming, sampling): +USD 200-500 setup, spread across the order

For first-time private label brands, starting with a stock design saves 1-2 weeks of sampling time and ~USD 500 in set-up.

## What's NOT in this price

The USD 62 FOB Tianjin price above is the **factory gate price**. It does NOT include:
- **Ocean freight** (USD 2-4 per piece for FCL to EU/US, depending on volume)
- **Customs duties** (US: 0% for cashmere under HTS 6110; EU: 12% VAT + 4% duty)
- **Import handling** (USD 0.50-1.50 per piece for customs broker + drayage)
- **Marketing / brand investment** (varies wildly)
- **Retail markup** (typically 2.5-4x wholesale for DTC brands)

So a USD 62 FOB cashmere sweater lands at the US port for about **USD 67-70 all-in**. A DTC brand selling at USD 280 retail has a 76% gross margin at this landed cost — before marketing, photography, customer service, returns.

## Why "USD 8 cashmere" is not a real product

You'll see sweaters marketed as "cashmere" at USD 8-15 on Amazon, fast-fashion sites, and discount platforms. These are almost always:
- **Recycled cashmere** (shoddy pulled from old garments, re-spun — short fibers, pills within 2 wears)
- **Cashmere/wool blend** (sometimes 70% wool, 30% cashmere — the label just says "cashmere blend")
- **Grade C cashmere** (16.5-19μm — coarser, scratchier, often blended with 20-30% viscose to soften the hand feel)
- **Mislabeled** (no actual cashmere content; Chinese or Turkish acrylic marketed as "cashmere-like")

If the price seems too good to be true, it isn't cashmere. Use the cost breakdown above as a sanity check: any "cashmere sweater" priced below USD 25 FOB equivalent is, at best, 30% cashmere with the rest being wool or synthetic.

## How to negotiate price without losing quality

The right way to bring the cost down is **not** to push the factory on margin (which compresses their ability to maintain quality), but to:
1. **Increase order quantity** (500 → 2,000+ pieces saves ~15%)
2. **Choose a more efficient gauge** (12gg → 14gg saves ~10% on fiber)
3. **Use Grade B instead of Grade A** (saves ~30% on fiber, hand feel still excellent)
4. **Combine SKUs in one production run** (amortizes set-up across more pieces)
5. **Book production 60+ days in advance** (better yarn pricing, factory capacity discounts)

A factory that drops price by 30% in negotiation is signaling they'll cut corners on fiber, labor, or quality control. The honest factory will explain the trade-offs and let you choose.
