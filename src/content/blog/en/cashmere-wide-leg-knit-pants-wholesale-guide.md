# Cashmere Wide-Leg Knit Pants: B2B Wholesale Guide (Yarn, Gauge, Fit, MOQ)

This guide walks through the engineering and sourcing decisions that determine whether a knit cashmere or wool-blend wide-leg pants program succeeds at your retail tier. It is written from the perspective of an Ordos facility that produces knit trousers as part of its full cashmere collection.

## Why Knit Pants vs Woven Trousers

Knit construction delivers four benefits over woven for premium bottoms:

- **Drape**: knit fabric flows vertically and recovers horizontally, creating the relaxed wide-leg silhouette without bulk
- **Comfort**: knit has built-in stretch, eliminating the need for elastane in most constructions
- **Hand-feel**: cashmere knit against the leg feels softer than equivalent woven cashmere
- **Packability**: knit pants pack smaller for travel retail

The trade-off is **structure**. Knit pants don't hold a crease the way woven trousers do. For buyers targeting office-wear or formalwear programs, woven is still the right choice. For buyers targeting loungewear, athleisure-tier, or weekend luxury, knit wins.

## Gauge and Construction

**Gauge 7gg – 9gg (chunky)**: visible knit texture, drapes heavily, loungewear aesthetic. Best for cashmere wide-leg programs sold at premium retail.

**Gauge 10gg – 12gg (mid)**: smooth surface, balanced structure, fits both casual and smart-casual programs. Standard for commercial knit trousers.

**Gauge 14gg (fine)**: refined drape, more formal aesthetic, requires finer yarn. Common for merino wool programs; rarely used for cashmere in knit pants.

**Construction method**: fully fashioned knit (knit to shape, panels linked together) is the standard for premium knit pants. Cut-and-sew knit (knit fabric panels cut and assembled like woven) is more common at lower price points and offers more pattern flexibility.

## Fit Block: Wide-Leg vs Straight vs Tapered

For FW 2026, the dominant B2B programs are wide-leg (relaxed, full leg opening), straight-leg (slim through hip and leg), and barrel-leg (curved taper at ankle).

**Wide-leg knit pants**: hip 110–125cm, leg opening 28–34cm, inseam 76–82cm. The most-requested fit for cashmere programs. Drapes elegantly when paired with cropped cashmere sweaters or fitted mock necks.

**Straight-leg knit pants**: hip 96–110cm, leg opening 22–26cm, inseam 76–80cm. More office-appropriate, suits merino wool programs.

**Barrel-leg / balloon knit pants**: gathered waist and ankle, exaggerated curve. A fashion-forward choice for boutique programs.

## Waistband and Closure

Three waistband options for knit pants:

**Elasticated waistband**: simplest, most comfortable, $0.80–1.20 added cost. Common for loungewear and athleisure-tier cashmere programs.

**Knitted rib waistband with drawcord**: more polished, drawcord internal or external. $1.50–2.80 added cost. Standard for premium retail.

**Set-in waistband with zipper and hook closure**: dressiest, requires woven waistband insertion. $3.50–5.50 added cost. Common for office-wear programs.

For wide-leg cashmere programs, the rib waistband with internal drawcord is the most-requested option.

## Pockets and Details

## Common Mistakes B2B Buyers Make

**Mistake 1: Specifying a 14gg cashmere pant.** The yarn is too thin to deliver the drape benefit. Use 7gg – 12gg for cashmere wide-leg programs.

**Mistake 2: Skipping recovery testing.** Knit pants recover from stretch differently depending on yarn twist. Always request a 50-stretch recovery test on the bulk production sample before shipping.

**Mistake 3: Underestimating inseam length variation.** Knit pants inseams stretch 5–8% under body weight during wear. A 76cm flat inseam may ride up to 78–80cm in wear. Build this into the spec.

**Mistake 4: Forgetting to specify pocket depth.** Side-seam welt pockets on wide-leg pants tend to open wider than intended. Specify pocket depth (typically 14–16cm) and pocket bag width (typically 18–20cm) explicitly.
