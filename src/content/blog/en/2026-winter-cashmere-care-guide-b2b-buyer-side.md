The honest reason cashmere gets a "high maintenance" reputation is operator error, not the fiber. The cashmere goat has been growing a fine undercoat that survives a 4,000-meter Inner Mongolian winter for ten million years — your customer's sweater does not fail in the wash because cashmere is delicate. It fails because someone washed it at 40°C with regular detergent, tossed it in the dryer, and then complained on TikTok.

This page is the buyer-side reference we hand to our own retail buyers and brand clients. Use it in your hangtag, your product detail page, your email followup, or your wholesale catalog insert. Every recommendation comes from the quality control bench at our Ordos facility — not from a marketing department.

## 1. The single rule that prevents 90% of cashmere complaints

**Cold water. Always. No exceptions.**

Hand wash at **20°C (68°F) or below** with a pH-neutral wool detergent (we use Eucalan and Soak in our own sample room; Eucalan is widely available in EU/US, Soak in CA). Machine wash is acceptable only on **delicate/wool cycle, cold water only, in a mesh laundry bag**. Hot water — anything above 30°C — permanently damages the cuticle scales and causes irreversible shrinkage. This is the number-one cause of returns we see across all client lots.

A useful rule of thumb: **if your hand feels cold, the water is fine. If your hand feels warm, the water is too hot.** Cashmere fibers felt and shrink at temperatures where wool and cotton are still comfortable.

## 2. Detergent — the second cause of complaints

Do not use:
- Regular laundry detergent (Tide, Persil, Ariel, etc.) — contains enzymes that digest cashmere's protein structure
- Bleach or any chlorine-based whitener
- Fabric softener (coats fibers in a film that masks the natural softness and traps odor)
- Woolite original — too alkaline for fine cashmere, even though the marketing says otherwise

Use:
- **pH-neutral wool/cashmere wash**: Eucalan, Soak, or any brand that lists "no rinse" or "neutral pH"
- **Baby shampoo** as a fallback in markets where wool wash is hard to source — it is pH-neutral and contains no enzymes. Use about 1 teaspoon per 2 liters of water.
- **Cashmere comb + pumice stone** for pilling, not a razor (razors cut too close and create thin spots)

## 3. Hand wash procedure — 7 steps your retailer can print on the hangtag

1. Fill basin with cold water (20°C max). Add wool wash per directions.
2. **Turn the garment inside out.** This protects the outer face from friction pilling.
3. Submerge and press down gently to release air. **Do not agitate, twist, wring, or rub.** Soak 15 minutes maximum.
4. Lift out, support the weight with both hands. Press (do not wring) water out against the side of the basin.
5. Roll in a clean dry towel, press to absorb excess water. Replace towel, repeat if very wet.
6. **Lay flat to dry on a second clean towel**, reshaping to original dimensions. Never hang (stretches shoulders) and never tumble dry (heat felting).
7. Air dry away from direct sunlight. Direct heat and UV degrade fiber color and weaken the yarn.

A 200g cashmere sweater will dry flat in 18–24 hours depending on humidity. A 600g blanket takes 36–48 hours. Tell the end-consumer to plan around this — it is not a 1-hour dryer run.

## 4. Pilling — what is normal and what is not

**Pilling in the first 2–4 wears is normal** and is a function of loose surface fibers working their way out of the yarn. This is especially common under the arms, on inner sleeves, and on the side panels of fitted garments where two fabric layers rub together. It does not indicate poor quality.

**Persistent pilling after 5–6 wears with no improvement** indicates either: (a) the yarn is under-twisted (lower-cost producer issue), or (b) the knit gauge is too loose for the yarn count. Both are upstream quality issues, not consumer care failures. We audit both at our QC bench — yarn twist per cm and stitch density per 10 cm — before any production lot is released.

For ongoing care, provide the end-consumer with a **cashmere comb** (€1–2 retail cost, can be branded) and instructions: lay the garment flat, comb gently in one direction, do this once after every 3–4 wears. Pilling stops once the loose surface fibers are removed.

## 5. Storage — the most common reason cashmere "shrinks" in a customer's closet

Cashmere does not shrink from being stored. It shrinks from being stored in a damp closet (mildew + compression), or stored in a sealed plastic bag (trapped moisture + trapped body oils → permanent yellowing), or stored on a hanger (shoulder stretch over time).

Correct storage:
- **Clean before storing.** Body oils and food residue oxidize over months and turn yellow. Recommend a wash before seasonal storage.
- **Breathable cotton storage bag** (not plastic, not vacuum). A pillowcase works.
- Add a **cedar block or lavender sachet** to deter moths. Mothballs smell and are toxic.
- **Fold, never hang.** Even heavy knits stretch at the shoulder when hung.
- **Avoid direct sunlight** in long-term storage — fades color.

## 6. Pilling, snags, and small holes — the repair conversation

Buyers sometimes ask how to handle small holes or pulled threads in cashmere. Two facts that are worth telling retailers in advance:

- **A pulled thread is not a hole.** Cashmere is knitted, and a single pulled thread can be coaxed back into the fabric with a tapestry needle. We provide 1-of-1 video instructions to wholesale clients.
- **A small moth hole can be invisibly mended** by any tailor who works with knit fabric. It is not a defect that requires replacement.

Both situations are about 100× less common than consumers think. The "cashmere falls apart" reputation is overwhelmingly a care-and-storage issue, not a quality issue.

## 7. Refreshing cashmere between washes (the 80% case)

Cashmere does not need to be washed after every wear. The natural antibacterial properties of the fiber (lanolin residue + protein structure) resist odor much better than cotton or synthetics. Recommend to end-consumers:

- **Air out for 24 hours** between wears (a chair back, not a closet)
- **Spot-clean** small marks with a damp cloth and wool wash, do not submerge
- **Wash every 5–7 wears**, or once per season for low-use items

This single recommendation will reduce customer complaints about "delicate cashmere" by more than half.
